## [1] "Why can't I come on molly?" — MDMA and orgasm/erection
- **Queries:** "why can't i orgasm on molly", "mdma can't cum", "molly makes me numb down there", "does mdma cause erectile dysfunction", "pillhead dick", "mdma sex drive"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** MEL Magazine (2019 lad-mag piece, no citations), Go Ask Alice (thin, dated), and addictionresource.net (rehab funnel — pivots to "is molly addictive?"). The actual research is paywalled at ScienceDirect/OUP and nobody has translated it. Nothing anywhere states the honest split: **desire up, function down**, which is precisely the paradox people are searching to explain.
- **Our honest answer:** This is the norm, not a personal defect. In the only controlled-ish human survey of the question (Zemishlany 2001), desire and satisfaction rose in >90% of users while erection was impaired in ~40% of men and orgasm was delayed in both sexes but reported as more intense when it did occur. Two mechanisms are proposed and neither is proven: (a) MDMA causes a large prolactin surge with kinetics resembling the post-orgasmic refractory state (Passie 2005 — this is an explicitly-labelled *hypothesis* paper, and no trial has tested prolactin blockade); (b) serotonergic inhibition plus sympathetic (vasoconstrictive) tone opposes the parasympathetic outflow erection and lubrication require. Note the harm-reduction consequence nobody mentions: the standard fix people reach for is a PDE5 inhibitor or a bump of coke, both of which stack cardiovascular load on top of MDMA. Also: reduced sensation plus increased desire plus delayed orgasm means longer intercourse with less feedback, i.e. a real friction-injury and condom-failure risk. That is the practical safety payload.
- **Evidence:** PMID 11311178 (Zemishlany 2001, *Eur Psychiatry*, "Subjective effects of MDMA ('Ecstasy') on human sexual function") — verified. PMID 15780482 (Passie 2005, *Med Hypotheses*, prolactin post-orgasmic-state hypothesis) — verified, and must be labelled a hypothesis paper. No RCT has tested MDMA's effect on sexual function; state that plainly.
- **Links to:** mdma-dosing-guide, mdma-comedown, cocaine-and-mdma, poppers-viagra-cialis (the PDE5 warning), condoms/sexual-health angle
- **Product tie-in:** none direct (do not sell ED products). Condom/lube mention only if the site does that elsewhere.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — high-volume, universally experienced, universally unaddressed, and it hides a genuine cardiovascular safety decision.

## [2] "Can MDMA make you unable to pee?"
- **Queries:** "can't pee on molly", "mdma urinary retention", "why can't i pee after mdma", "molly makes it hard to pee", "shy bladder mdma"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** Almost nothing. Search returns case reports behind paywalls plus, bizarrely, postpartum-urinary-retention papers. No harm reduction site covers it. Reddit threads exist with confident wrong answers ("you're dehydrated, drink more water") — which is the single most dangerous possible advice here, because MDMA already drives SIADH and hyponatremia.
- **Our honest answer:** Yes, and difficulty urinating on MDMA is common and usually benign — alpha-adrenergic stimulation tightens the bladder neck, the same mechanism that makes stimulants cause "shy bladder". It resolves as the drug wears off. The critical correction: **do not respond by drinking more water.** Not passing urine on MDMA is more often bladder-neck spasm plus antidiuresis (MDMA raises vasopressin/ADH), not dehydration; force-drinking on top of that is the exact pathway to fatal hyponatremia. Escalate to hospital if you genuinely cannot void for 8+ hours *and* have lower abdominal pain or a palpable bladder — true acute retention needs a catheter, not more fluid. Chronic heavy use has produced neurogenic bladder in case reports, but that is rare and not what a weekend user is experiencing.
- **Evidence:** PMID 12614266 (Inman & Greene 2003, *BJU Int*, "The agony and the ecstasy: acute urinary retention after MDMA abuse") — verified. PMID 18570171 (Zhu 2008, *J Med Toxicol*, "Neurogenic bladder and chronic urinary retention associated with MDMA abuse") — verified. Both are **case reports**; there is no incidence data at all, so we cannot say how common it is. Say so.
- **Links to:** mdma-and-your-period (hyponatremia section), festival-heat-hydration, mdma-dosing-guide, ketamine-bladder-damage (contrast: different mechanism, different drug)
- **Product tie-in:** none
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — near-zero competition, embarrassing enough that people only ask anonymously, and the intuitive folk answer is actively lethal.

## [3] "How do I look after my nose if I snort coke?" (and: is Vaseline safe up there?)
- **Queries:** "coke nose", "how to heal nose after cocaine", "vaseline for coke nose", "saline rinse after snorting", "nose bleeding after coke", "how to snort without damaging nose", "does my septum have a hole"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** Checked live. Page 1 is Daily Mail scare copy ("woman's nose collapsed"), four rehab-admissions blogs (paxmemphis, recoveryindianapolis, orlandorecovery, choosehelp), a plastic-surgery lead-gen site selling reconstruction, and Healthline's "Vaseline for coke nose". Every one of them answers the *damage-already-done* question and pivots to treatment or surgery. **Nobody answers the actual question people type**, which is preventive and routine: what do I do tonight and tomorrow morning so this doesn't happen. That is a pure moralising-vs-practical gap.
- **Our honest answer:** Three parts. (1) The prevention routine that no ranked page gives: grind to a genuinely fine powder (crystals are what lacerate), alternate nostrils, never share and never use a banknote (see our existing hep C post), and rinse with **isotonic saline** afterwards — not water, which stings and swells the mucosa. (2) The correctable popular claim: **do not pack petroleum jelly or Vaseline inside your nose**, which is exactly what the top-ranked Healthline result is about. Intranasal petrolatum is a documented cause of exogenous lipoid pneumonia because it is aspirated over time, and it is not a mucosal healer. Use a water-based saline gel instead. (3) The honest limits: saline, humidification and abstinence let inflamed mucosa recover, but **an established septal perforation does not close on its own and will slowly enlarge** — no rinse, spray, or supplement reverses it, and anyone claiming otherwise is selling something. Also worth naming: cocaine-induced midline destructive lesions are strongly associated with levamisole adulteration, which ties straight into our existing levamisole post and makes testing a real (not decorative) mitigation.
- **Evidence:** PMID 7924535 (*Chest* 1994, "Lipoid pneumonia due to intranasal application of petroleum jelly. An old problem revisited") — verified. PMID 8131586 (*Chest* 1994, "Exogenous lipoid pneumonia due to nasal application of petroleum jelly") — verified. Both are case-based; there is **no controlled trial of any nasal-care routine in people who snort cocaine**, so the rinse/grind advice must be presented as mechanistic reasoning and ENT practice, not proven protocol. Say "none exists" for the routine itself.
- **Links to:** cocaine-harm-reduction, levamisole-in-cocaine, sharing-straws-hepatitis-c, how-to-test-cocaine
- **Product tie-in:** saline nasal rinse / isotonic spray, water-based nasal gel (Amazon); snorting straws are a judgement call. DanceSafe kit link via the levamisole angle.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — big query family, entirely owned by rehab and surgery funnels, and the single most-searched practical sub-question ("Vaseline") currently has an answer we can beat with two verified citations.

## [4] "Coke dick": why cocaine kills your erection, and why the obvious fix is the dangerous part
- **Queries:** "coke dick", "cocaine erectile dysfunction", "why can't i get hard on coke", "cocaine and sex", "does coke dick go away", "viagra and cocaine", "sextasy"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** Checked live via WebSearch. The page is split between rehab funnels (rehab4addiction, thebalance.clinic) and **telehealth companies that sell ED medication** (Pilot, Doctronic, medicalphysics.institute). The second group is the problem: their commercial incentive is to answer "yes, and here is a sildenafil prescription", and not one of them flags that combining a PDE5 inhibitor with a sympathomimetic vasoconstrictor is a cardiovascular decision. Rehab sites, meanwhile, give the moralised version ("this is a sign of addiction") and no mechanism. Nobody answers "does it go away", which is the actual anxious question.
- **Our honest answer:** Acute erectile failure on cocaine is expected pharmacology, not a personal signal: cocaine is a vasoconstrictor, and erection requires vasodilation and parasympathetic tone — the drug is doing the exact opposite of what an erection needs, while simultaneously raising desire via dopamine. That mismatch (wanting sex, body refusing) is the whole phenomenon. Acute effects resolve as the drug clears. The honest uncertainty: whether *chronic* use causes lasting ED is **not established in humans** — the mechanistic work is a rat model, and the human data are cross-sectional surveys of polydrug-dependent treatment populations, which cannot separate cocaine from alcohol, opioids, depression, or sleep loss. Anyone stating a percentage is making it up. Two things that are worth naming and that no ranking page does: (a) the standard self-fix is a PDE5 inhibitor on top of cocaine, stacking a vasodilator against a vasoconstrictor while heart rate and blood pressure are already elevated — the same physiological argument as our poppers post, and the reason "sextasy" shows up in ED presentations; (b) cocaine is a recognised cause of **ischaemic priapism**, which is the opposite failure mode and a genuine emergency — an erection lasting over 4 hours needs an ED visit within hours to avoid permanent damage. That single fact is worth the whole post.
- **Evidence:** PMID 17420087 (*Eur Urol* 2007, "Peripheral mechanisms of erectile dysfunction in a rat model of chronic cocaine use") — verified; **animal model, must be labelled as such**. PMID 28471320 (*Subst Use Misuse* 2017, "Erectile Dysfunction Symptoms in Polydrug Dependents Seeking Treatment") — verified; cross-sectional, treatment-seeking sample, no causal claim available. PMID 38369291 (*Actas Urol Esp* 2024, "Cocaine-related ischemic priapism. Systematic review and presentation of a single center series") — verified. **No controlled human trial of cocaine and erectile function exists, and no study has tested cocaine + PDE5 co-use.** State both.
- **Links to:** cocaine-heart-attack, cocaine-and-alcohol, cocaine-and-mdma, poppers-viagra-cialis, cocaine-harm-reduction
- **Product tie-in:** none — deliberately. Refusing to sell an ED fix here is the differentiator against everything that ranks.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — very large query, currently answered by people selling the risky fix, and it contains a true emergency (priapism) that no ranked page mentions.

## [5] "I wet myself on G" — incontinence during a GHB blackout, and what it actually tells you
- **Queries:** "ghb wet myself", "peed myself on g", "ghb incontinence", "is it normal to piss yourself on ghb", "g nap wet the bed", "gbl bedwetting"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** Effectively nothing. PubMed surfaces only sodium-oxybate (Xyrem) adverse-event literature and unrelated urology papers. Harm reduction orgs cover GHB dosing and overdose but skip this entirely — it is the textbook "embarrassing question established orgs omit". The only places it is discussed are forum threads, where the dominant reply is reassurance ("happens to everyone, don't worry"), which is exactly the wrong lesson.
- **Our honest answer:** It happens, and it is not a sign that something rare or shameful went wrong with you — but the reassurance answer is dangerous because it obscures what incontinence actually indicates. Losing bladder control during a "G-nap" means you were **deeply unconscious**, past the point of protective reflexes. That is not sleep; it is a dose-dependent coma, and the same depth of unconsciousness that lets the bladder go also removes the gag reflex. The lethal event in GHB is almost never the drug itself — it is vomiting while unrousable and aspirating, or airway obstruction from position. So the correct read is: incontinence = you overdosed, recalibrate the dose downward, and never sleep it off alone or on your back. The second thing to name honestly: incontinence *plus* rhythmic jerking, tongue-biting, or a prolonged confused period afterwards is a seizure differential, not just a deep G-nap, and that changes the disposition (our can-ghb-cause-seizures post is the internal handoff). Useful calibration for the "is it the drug or is it me" anxiety: in the RCT meta-analysis of therapeutic sodium oxybate — pharmaceutical GHB, dosed at night, in bed — enuresis was numerically raised but **not statistically distinguishable from placebo** (RR 2.6, 95% CI 0.8–9.8). Which is the point: at medical doses it is a marginal side effect; at recreational doses it is a depth-of-coma marker.
- **Evidence:** PMID 22893778 (*J Clin Sleep Med* 2012, "Sodium oxybate for narcolepsy with cataplexy: systematic review and meta-analysis") — verified; abstract explicitly reports "Enuresis was not significantly different from placebo (n = 2, RR: 2.6, 95% CI: 0.8, 9.8)". PMID 34860897 (*J Pharm Technol* 2014, sodium oxybate + ethanol coma case) — verified; the case presented with GCS 7, apnoea, **and urinary incontinence**, which is precisely the association we are describing. **No study has measured the incidence of incontinence in recreational GHB use — none exists.** Say so.
- **Links to:** ghb-dosing-guide, ghb-and-alcohol, can-ghb-cause-seizures, ghb-withdrawal, what-not-to-mix-with-alcohol
- **Product tie-in:** oral dosing syringes (already used on the dosing post) — the honest tie-in, since the fix is dose precision.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — zero competition, high shame value so people search it privately, and it converts a humiliating anecdote into a correctly-understood overdose signal.

## [6] "My B12 test came back normal but my hands are still numb" — the nitrous test that doesn't work
- **Queries:** "b12 test normal but numb hands whippets", "nitrous b12 blood test normal", "do i need a b12 test after nitrous", "tingling after whippets but tests fine", "how do i know if nitrous damaged my nerves", "i take b12 so is nitrous safe"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** The entire internet — harm reduction sites, Reddit, and our own existing nitrous post included — converges on one piece of advice: "take B12 and get your B12 levels checked." That advice is **wrong in a way that actively harms people**, and no page anywhere corrects it. Someone with early nitrous myeloneuropathy gets a normal serum B12, is reassured, keeps using, and presents months later with subacute combined degeneration of the cord. This is the single clearest "popular answer is actively wrong" gap I found across all six clusters.
- **Our honest answer:** Nitrous does not deplete B12; it **irreversibly oxidises the cobalt core of the B12 you already have**, inactivating it. The molecule is still in your blood and still counted by a standard serum B12 assay. So a normal B12 result does not rule out nitrous nerve injury. The hard number: in a hospital cohort of 31 patients admitted with nitrous-related neurological damage, only 17 of 31 had serum B12 below the lab's lower limit — **nearly half tested normal** — while the functional markers were grossly abnormal in the group (median homocysteine 87.7 µmol/L against a normal range under 15; median methylmalonic acid 3.8 µmol/L against under 0.5). The tests to ask for are **methylmalonic acid and homocysteine**, not serum B12, and you should say this to the GP explicitly because they will not think of it for a 22-year-old. Second correction, equally important: taking B12 supplements does not make nitrous safe. There is a published case of subacute combined degeneration developing **despite the patient supplementing B12 on their own**, because supplementation does not outrun ongoing inactivation. Third, the honest uncertainty: there is no validated threshold of nitrous exposure that is "safe", no evidence for a prophylactic dose, and recovery after injury is variable and often incomplete — anyone quoting a canister count is inventing it.
- **Evidence:** PMID 37917184 (*Clin Toxicol* 2023, "Functional vitamin B12 deficiency is a consistent feature in hospital admissions for neurological disorders due to the use of nitrous oxide") — verified, abstract contains all the figures quoted above; note it carries a published erratum (Clin Toxicol 2024;62(1):67) which should be checked before quoting. PMID 35253567 (*Clin Toxicol* 2022, "Nitrous oxide abuse induced subacute combined degeneration despite patient initiated B12 supplementation") — verified. PMID 12967021 (*WMJ* 2003, "Myeloneuropathy from nitrous oxide abuse: unusually high methylmalonic acid and homocysteine levels") — verified, useful as the older independent confirmation. PMID 39940065 (*Neurol Res Pract* 2025, severe SCD case series) — verified. **No trial has tested any prophylactic or treatment protocol for nitrous-induced B12 inactivation — none exists.**
- **Links to:** nitrous-oxide-b12-nerve-damage, galaxy-gas-nitrous-tanks-nerve-damage; and this is the natural anchor for the already-logged "How to recover B12 after nitrous" idea — **note the overlap: this post is the *diagnostic* question, that one is the *recovery protocol*. Keep them distinct or merge deliberately, don't publish both blind.**
- **Product tie-in:** methylcobalamin (honest, but must be framed as "this does not make it safe"). At-home MMA/homocysteine test panels if any reputable one exists — verify before linking.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — the strongest correctable-wrong-answer in my clusters, uncontested, hard-cited, and directly adjacent to a real medical decision.

## [7] "K-cramps: is that my bladder or my liver?" — ketamine cholangiopathy
- **Queries:** "k cramps", "what causes k cramps", "ketamine stomach pain right side", "ketamine liver damage", "ketamine cramps under ribs", "why does ketamine hurt my stomach", "does ketamine damage your liver"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** Every ranking answer, ours included, treats "K-cramps" as a urinary-tract phenomenon — bladder and kidney. The community folk model is that cramps are the bladder complaining. That model is incomplete in a consequential way, and the correction is not on page one anywhere: a substantial share of upper-abdominal ketamine pain is **biliary**, from ketamine-associated cholangiopathy (bile-duct dilatation and stricturing, sometimes with a picture resembling sclerosing cholangitis). Our own ketamine-bladder-damage post explains the norketamine-in-urine mechanism well but has **no liver or biliary content at all** — I checked; the word "biliary" does not appear in any ketamine post on the site. So this is both an external gap and an internal one.
- **Our honest answer:** "K-cramps" is a folk term covering at least two different organ systems. Lower abdominal/pelvic pain with urgency and frequency is uropathy. **Pain in the upper right abdomen, under the ribs, sometimes radiating to the back or shoulder blade, is a different problem** — that is the biliary presentation, and it needs liver function tests and imaging (ultrasound or MRCP), not a urologist and not more water. Two things make this dangerous in practice. First, ketamine is an analgesic, so the reflex response to K-cramps is to take more ketamine, which relieves the symptom while worsening the cause — a self-reinforcing loop that no ranked page names. Second, presenting to an ED with right-upper-quadrant pain and no disclosed ketamine history gets you worked up for gallstones, which you probably do not have. Disclosure changes the workup. The honest limits: the evidence base here is **case reports and case series, systematically reviewed but never a cohort with denominators**, so we cannot say what fraction of users are affected or at what exposure. Reversibility on cessation is reported but inconsistent. Anyone giving you a threshold dose is making it up.
- **Evidence:** PMID 38431685 (*J Med Case Rep* 2024, "Features of biliary tract diseases in ketamine abusers: a systematic review of case reports") — verified; note the design in the post, it is a systematic review **of case reports**, not of trials. PMID 40017428 (*Histopathology* 2025, "Ketamine cholangiopathy: clinical features and liver biopsy findings") — verified. PMID 37267048 (*Clin Toxicol* 2023, "The clinical toxicology of ketamine") — verified, good general-authority anchor. **No prospective cohort has established incidence, dose-response, or reversibility — none exists.**
- **Links to:** ketamine-bladder-damage, is-ketamine-addictive, ketamine-and-alcohol, ketamine-nasal-spray. **Also flags an existing-page fix:** add a biliary section to ketamine-bladder-damage or cross-link explicitly, since that post currently teaches the incomplete model.
- **Product tie-in:** none honest here. Milligram scale / nasal spray kit only as frequency-reduction tools.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — a genuinely different answer to a very common community term, no competition, and it changes what someone says to a doctor.

## [8] "Do poppers actually relax you for anal sex, or is that just what everyone says?"
- **Queries:** "do poppers relax the anus", "how do poppers help bottoming", "do poppers actually work for anal", "poppers before anal sex", "why do gay men use poppers", "poppers and douching"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** This is the reason most poppers are bought, and it is almost entirely absent from harm reduction writing. Mainstream drug sites answer "what are poppers" chemically and stop at the doorstep of the actual use case. LGBTQ health sites cover HIV/PrEP context but rarely the mechanism. Vendor sites assert it flatly and enthusiastically, because they are selling the bottle. So the field is: chemistry pages that dodge the question, and sellers who answer it in whatever way moves product. Textbook territory for us.
- **Our honest answer:** The mechanism is real and the specific claim is untested — and those are two different statements that everyone conflates. Alkyl nitrites are nitric-oxide donors, and nitric oxide genuinely relaxes the internal anal sphincter: that is not folklore, it is the basis of an established medical treatment, topical glyceryl trinitrate for anal fissure, which has Cochrane-reviewed trial evidence behind it. Systemic GTN measurably reduces anal sphincter tone in humans. Even sildenafil relaxes isolated human internal anal sphincter tissue in vitro. So the pharmacological story is coherent. **But no study has ever tested whether inhaled poppers relax the anal sphincter, at what dose, or for how long.** The honest answer is "the mechanism is plausible and clinically exploited by a different nitrate, and the specific claim about poppers has never been measured." Then the harm reduction payload that actually matters, none of which vendors mention: the effect is short (seconds to a couple of minutes) so people redose repeatedly during sex, which is where the PDE5 interaction and the methaemoglobinaemia risk compound; reduced sensation plus reduced sphincter tone means **you can be injured without feeling it**, so pain is a less reliable stop signal than usual; and poppers are strongly associated with reduced condom use, which is a real epidemiological finding and not moralising. Lube and going slowly do the same job with none of the cardiovascular cost.
- **Evidence:** PMID 22336789 (*Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2012, "Non surgical therapy for anal fissure") — verified; this is the anchor for "NO donors relax the internal sphincter and this is medicine, not folklore". PMID 28905197 (*Ir J Med Sci* 2018, "Systemic glyceryl trinitrate reduces anal sphincter tone: is there a therapeutic indication?") — verified. PMID 17335124 (*Br J Surg* 2007, "Relaxation of the isolated human internal anal sphincter by sildenafil") — verified; in vitro, label it. **On poppers specifically for this purpose: no controlled study exists. Say exactly that.**
- **Links to:** poppers-viagra-cialis, isopropyl-poppers-eye-damage (once written), the methaemoglobinaemia post below, sharing-straws-hepatitis-c (bottle-sharing angle)
- **Product tie-in:** none, and refusing one is the point. Do not link poppers vendors.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — the highest-intent poppers query there is, owned entirely by sellers, and it is a clean showcase of "mechanism real, specific claim untested".

## [9] "Why did my lips go blue after poppers?" — methaemoglobinaemia, and what happens if you swallow them
- **Queries:** "poppers blue lips", "blue fingers after poppers", "i drank poppers by accident", "swallowed poppers what do i do", "poppers oxygen levels", "poppers grey skin", "can poppers kill you"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** Vendor FAQs say "never swallow poppers" with no explanation of what swallowing does, which makes it read like boilerplate rather than a warning about a specific, fast, treatable emergency. General drug sites list "poppers can cause methaemoglobinaemia" as a term with no clinical picture attached. Nobody tells the reader the two things they need at 3am: **what it looks like**, and **that the treatment exists and is quick if you get there**.
- **Our honest answer:** Alkyl nitrites oxidise the iron in haemoglobin so it can no longer carry oxygen. The signature is skin, lips, or nail beds going blue, grey, or slate-coloured **while the person is breathing normally and does not feel short of breath in proportion to how they look** — that mismatch is the tell. Two additional facts that are genuinely decision-relevant and appear nowhere on page one. First, **a pulse oximeter will lie to you**: methaemoglobinaemia pins SpO2 readings around the mid-80s regardless of the true oxygen content, so a "not that bad" phone-clip reading is meaningless here. Second, oxygen alone does not fix it — the antidote is intravenous methylene blue, it works within minutes, and it is only available in hospital. So the correct action is to go, not to wait and watch. Swallowing rather than inhaling is the high-risk route by a wide margin, and there is a published near-fatal case; ingestion is usually accidental (a bottle mistaken for a drink, or a drink contaminated with one) and is a genuine reason to keep bottles capped, labelled, and off the table. Worth adding: intravenous use of poppers is a reported and catastrophic route. Also flag that people with G6PD deficiency are a special case, because methylene blue is unsafe for them — which is a reason to tell the hospital your history rather than to avoid going.
- **Evidence:** PMID 39925540 (*Cureus* 2025, "Do Not Drink Poppers: A Case Report of Near Fatal Methemoglobinemia After Ingestion of Alkyl Nitrite") — verified. PMID 37247947 (*BMJ Case Rep* 2023, "Methaemoglobinaemia due to alkyl nitrites in a patient with suspected traumatic injuries") — verified. PMID 32188665 (*Clin Med (Lond)* 2020, "Lessons of the month 3: Intravenous poppers abuse: case report, management and possible complications") — verified. All case reports; **there is no incidence data for methaemoglobinaemia from ordinary inhaled recreational use — none exists**, and we should not imply it is common from sniffing.
- **Links to:** poppers-viagra-cialis, isopropyl-poppers-eye-damage, finding [8] above, popper-burns idea already in blog-ideas
- **Product tie-in:** none. Explicitly say a pulse oximeter is not a safety device here — that is a rare chance to *un*-sell something.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — real emergency, clean actionable answer, and the pulse-oximeter correction is the kind of specific, verifiable detail that gets cited.

## [10] "Do I stop my Adderall/Vyvanse before taking MDMA?"
- **Queries:** "adderall and molly", "can i take mdma on vyvanse", "should i skip my adderall before a rave", "concerta and mdma", "adhd meds and molly", "elvanse mdma", "does adderall ruin your roll"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** Nothing good, and the demand is enormous — a very large share of the target audience is prescribed a stimulant. What exists is Reddit threads split between "it does nothing, don't bother" and "it makes the roll way better", plus rehab pages that answer a different question (stimulant misuse) moralistically. Our own site has a strong mdma-ssri-interaction post and a why-didnt-my-molly-work post, and **neither addresses ADHD medication**, which is the single most common daily prescription in this audience. Internal gap as well as external.
- **Our honest answer:** This is one of the rare combination questions with an actual randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial in humans, and the result is unusually clean: methylphenidate plus MDMA **did not enhance the psychotropic effects of MDMA**, but the haemodynamic and adverse effects of the combination were significantly higher than either drug alone. All of the cardiovascular cost, none of the extra effect. That is the headline and it settles the Reddit argument with evidence rather than opinion. Important scope limits to state honestly: the trial used methylphenidate (Ritalin/Concerta), **not** amphetamine-class drugs (Adderall, Vyvanse/elvanse, dexamfetamine), and the two are different pharmacology — amphetamines are releasers like MDMA, so the interaction is plausibly *worse*, but that has not been tested, and we should say so rather than extrapolate silently. Also: non-stimulant ADHD medication is a separate question again — atomoxetine is a noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor and has its own theoretical concerns. Practical framing: this is a hyperthermia and cardiac-load question, not a "will it ruin my roll" question, which is what people think they are asking. The relevant decision is about the day's total stimulant burden in a hot room, and skipping a dose has a real cost too (driving, functioning, getting home safely) that a blanket "just don't" ignores.
- **Evidence:** PMID 24103254 (*Int J Neuropsychopharmacol* 2014, "Pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic effects of methylphenidate and MDMA administered alone or in combination") — verified; abstract explicitly states the combination did not enhance psychotropic effects but produced significantly higher haemodynamic and adverse effects, and that neither drug altered the other's pharmacokinetics (NCT01465685). **No controlled study of MDMA with amphetamine-class ADHD medication or with atomoxetine exists — state "none exists" and refuse to extrapolate a number.**
- **Links to:** mdma-ssri-interaction, why-didnt-my-molly-work, mdma-dosing-guide, festival-heat-hydration, cocaine-and-mdma (same cardiovascular-stacking logic)
- **Product tie-in:** none
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — huge prescribed audience, a live argument online, and we can end it with a real RCT. Rare combination in this field.

## [11] "Why does cocaine make me need the toilet immediately?" — and the version that is an emergency
- **Queries:** "why does cocaine make you poop", "coke poops", "cocaine diarrhea", "cocaine stomach pain", "cocaine makes me shit myself", "cocaine constipation after", "stomach cramps after coke"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** This is a genuinely high-volume query that exists almost entirely as a joke online — Reddit threads, listicles, no mechanism, no citations, and crucially no distinction between the banal version and the dangerous one. Rehab sites do not touch it because it is undignified. It is close to a perfect example of the site's target: embarrassing enough that serious sources skip it, common enough that everyone wants the answer, and it conceals a real emergency.
- **Our honest answer:** Two parts, and the split is the whole value of the post. **The common version is benign and boring:** stimulants increase gut motility and sympathetic outflow, and much street cocaine is heavily cut, so urgency shortly after a line is expected. That is not a sign of a bad batch or of anything wrong with you. **The uncommon version is a surgical emergency,** and the distinguishing features are worth memorising: severe, constant abdominal pain that is out of proportion to examination, pain that persists long after the drug has worn off, bloody stool, vomiting, or a rigid abdomen. Cocaine is a vasoconstrictor and the gut vasculature is not exempt — ischaemic colitis, bowel necrosis, perforation and cocaine-associated acute pancreatitis are all documented. This is the same mechanism as our existing cocaine-heart-attack post applied to a different organ bed, and that framing makes the internal link natural rather than forced. Two practical points nobody makes: pain that starts hours *after* use and does not settle is the pattern that should send you in, and — as with the chest-pain post — you must disclose cocaine use, because an ischaemic gut in a young person is otherwise not on the differential and delay is what causes the resection. Honest limits: these are case reports and case-based reviews; there is **no incidence data**, and this is rare relative to the number of people who use cocaine. Do not write it as though every stomach ache is necrosis.
- **Evidence:** PMID 26798523 (*Case Rep Gastrointest Med* 2015, "Sonographic and Endoscopic Findings in Cocaine-Induced Ischemic Colitis") — verified. PMID 38546011 (*J Investig Med High Impact Case Rep* 2024, "Cocaine Gut: A Rare Case of Cocaine-Induced Esophageal, Gastric, and Small Bowel Necrosis") — verified. PMID 36185891 (*Cureus* 2022, "Cocaine Use and Incarceration: A Rare Cause of Bowel Ischemia, Perforation, and Gastrointestinal Hemorrhage") — verified. PMID 33945068 (*Clin J Gastroenterol* 2021, "Acute pancreatitis secondary to cocaine use: a case-based systematic literature review") — verified. **No study has measured the frequency of ordinary GI urgency after cocaine — none exists**, so the benign half must be presented as mechanism plus experience, not data.

## [12] "I think I was spiked with G — how long do I have to get tested?"
- **Queries:** "how long does ghb stay in your system if spiked", "can they test for ghb", "drink spiked what do i do", "ghb urine test how long", "date rape drug test window", "how long is ghb detectable"
- **What ranks now and why it fails:** Two failure modes, both bad. Police, university and news pages repeat "GHB leaves the system in about 12 hours" as a soft generality with no source, no number, and — critically — **no urgency framing**, so the reader does not understand that the clock is the entire story. Meanwhile a huge volume of content treats GHB as *the* spiking drug, which distorts what people ask for and what gets tested. Nobody gives the reader the one thing that changes an outcome: go now, ask for the specific test, and here is what happens if you wait until morning.
- **Our honest answer:** The window is much shorter than people assume, and it is measured in hours. In a controlled human dosing study, using the standard 10 mg/L urine cut-off that separates exogenous GHB from the body's own endogenous GHB, **12.5% of samples were already below the cut-off at 3–6 hours, 81.3% were below it at 6–12 hours, and 100% of samples collected between 12 and 24 hours were below it** — undetectable. The authors' own conclusion is that the real detection window is shorter than the commonly quoted 12 hours. Practical translation, which is the whole post: waiting until the next morning to decide whether to get tested is, for GHB specifically, usually the same as deciding not to test. Blood is shorter still. Ask explicitly for a **urine sample collected as early as possible and preserved**, and say the words "I want it tested for GHB", because GHB is not on a standard hospital or workplace drug panel and will not be looked for unless requested. Two honest corrections that this post should carry rather than dodge. First, the reason GHB is hard to detect is not only the short half-life but that it is **endogenous** — everyone has a baseline, which is why a cut-off exists and why late samples become uninterpretable rather than merely negative. Second, and this matters for how people are believed: alcohol is overwhelmingly the substance most often involved in drug-facilitated assault, and a negative GHB test does not mean nothing happened. Framing the test as the arbiter of whether someone was assaulted is both scientifically wrong and harmful, and we should say so plainly.
- **Evidence:** PMID 16872565 (*J Anal Toxicol* 2006, "GHB urine concentrations after single-dose administration in humans") — verified; the 10 mg/L cut-off percentages above are quoted directly from the abstract, which also notes significant between-individual variability and sex differences in the first 3 hours. PMID 37343712 (*Regul Toxicol Pharmacol* 2023, "Gamma-hydroxybutyrate (GHB), 1,4-butanediol (1,4BD), and gamma-butyrolactone (GBL) intoxication: A state-of-the-art review") — verified, good general anchor. PMID 28869822 (*Forensic Sci Int* 2017, "Potential of GHB phase-II-metabolites to complement current approaches in GHB post administration detection") — verified; useful for the honest note that **extended-window metabolite methods are research-stage and not what a hospital will run**.
- **Links to:** ghb-dosing-guide, ghb-and-alcohol, can-ghb-cause-seizures, what-not-to-mix-with-alcohol, harm-reduction-at-festivals. Also the already-logged Good Samaritan post.
- **Product tie-in:** **none — do not monetise this page at all**, and do not link drink-spiking test strips, whose accuracy for GHB is poor and which would be actively harmful to endorse here. The refusal is itself the trust signal.
- **Opportunity:** HIGH — high-stakes, time-critical, and the specific numbers exist and are verifiable while every ranking page hand-waves. Handle tone carefully; this is the one post in the set where a wrong register does real damage.
