For 23 years, DrugsData (formerly EcstasyData) was the answer to "where can I get my drugs tested." You mailed a sample, paid a co-pay, and a licensed lab published an anonymous GC-MS result. That stopped on 10 April 2024, and it has not restarted. The trap is that the site still looks alive: the searchable results database of past samples is fully online, so people land on it, assume the service works, and never find the notice. This page lists what is actually operating, checked on 14 August 2026.

## Quick answers

**Is DrugsData accepting samples?** No. The homepage carries a banner reading "New Sample Submissions Are Not Being Accepted." Erowid Center's June 2026 update says they are working with legal counsel and that no change to DrugsData's status is expected earlier than 2027.

**Why did it stop?** The DEA ordered the lab to stop analysing DrugsData samples and re-apply for its exception to the rules that bar anonymous testing and the mailing of suspected controlled substances without a licence.

**Is the old results database still useful?** Yes, for historical reference and for looking up pill press markings, but the newest DrugsData-analysed samples are from early April 2024. They still republish results from other checking groups.

**What replaced it for mail-in testing in the US?** Transparency Testing, a commercial mail-in lab. It is not free and it is not run by a harm reduction nonprofit, but it was accepting samples on 14 August 2026.

**What should most people do instead?** Reagent kits plus fentanyl test strips at home. That is not as good as mass spectrometry, and it is the realistic default for the majority of people in the US right now.

## Mail-in lab analysis

**Transparency Testing (US, commercial, operating).** Mail-in analysis using FTIR, LC-MS, NMR and NIR. Their live order form on 14 August 2026 listed powders, pills, blotters and geltabs, mushroom products, cannabis, kratom, plant and cactus material, extracts and steroids. Powder and blotter panels ran from $40 for basic identification to $110 for identification plus purity; specialist panels such as natural and synthetic tryptamine screens cost more. They state they cannot currently accept liquids, they offer anonymous payment, and they say they delete customer data one month after results. DanceSafe's own site points people here for mail-in checking. The honest framing: this is a paid commercial lab, not a public-interest project, and the price is a real barrier for a lot of people.

**Energy Control (Spain, international, operating).** Has run a drug checking service since 1999 and an international mail-in service since 2014, using GC-MS and LC-MS. They identify most common substances plus adulterants, and quantify some. Note their own caveat: they cannot quantify substances submitted as unknowns.

**Get Your Drugs Tested (Vancouver, Canada, partially operating).** A free community analysis service using FTIR and test strips, with a public results database. On 14 August 2026 their site stated that the mail-in program had re-opened as of 1 August 2026, and that in-person storefront services would restart in late August. Check before travelling to the storefront.

## Community and public health drug checking

These are the services most likely to be free, and most are reached through a local harm reduction program rather than by mailing a sample yourself.

**UNC Street Drug Analysis Lab / streetsafe.supply (operating).** A public service of the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, running high resolution GC-MS on mailed samples, with all results published anonymously. Their results site was updated on 14 August 2026 and showed 23,533 completed samples from 42 states. They hold DEA authorisation, they state they do not work with law enforcement, and they collect no identifying information. They are built to serve harm reduction programs, health departments and user unions, so the route in is usually through a partner organisation. Note their own limitation: their method identifies what is present but does not give purity or quantification.

**StreetCheck and MADDS (operating).** StreetCheck is a Brandeis-run community drug checking project that standardises sample collection and reporting, with a public results and drug trends database and published test strip performance data. It began in Massachusetts as the Massachusetts Drug Supply Data Stream (MADDS) and now connects sites across the Northeast. Massachusetts agencies providing checking through MADDS do it free of charge. Samples need to be visible to the naked eye, about half a grain of rice; powders, rocks, crystals, pills, and once-used pipes, cookers and cottons are accepted; syringes are not, and plant material including mushrooms does not test well. Information lives at info.streetcheck.org. Do not rely on old links to madds.org, which did not resolve when checked.

**OnPoint NYC (operating).** On-site drug checking at their East Harlem and Washington Heights locations during business hours, run with the NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene. They use FTIR spectroscopy plus immunoassay strips for fentanyl, morphine, cocaine, benzodiazepines, xylazine, ketamine and methamphetamine, with secondary lab verification for quality assurance.

**CanTEST (Canberra, Australia, operating).** Free and confidential fixed-site checking delivered by Directions Health Services with CAHMA and Pill Testing Australia, funded by ACT Health. On 14 August 2026 the published hours were Thursdays 3pm to 6pm and Fridays 6pm to 9pm at the City Community Health Centre, 1 Moore Street, Canberra. Pills, capsules, powders, crystals, liquids and blotters are accepted; plant material and dilute solutions often are not. Directions published a third-year CanTEST report in February 2026. Note that CanTEST is Australian, not Canadian, which is a common mix-up.

## How to find something near you

There is no single reliable national map. Two starting points:

- **StreetCheck's service finder** maps drug checking locations across New England, with the honest note that not every pin offers real-time results.
- **The Harm Reduction Innovation Lab's directory of North American drug checking programs** is the widest-scope listing and is the one DrugsData itself points to. Its page was live on 14 August 2026, but the entries are not dated, so call a program before travelling to it.

For event-based checking, DanceSafe operates booths at many US festivals and The Loop runs on-site mass spectrometry at participating UK events. We cover how those work, what they detect, and the behavioural evidence behind them in [drug checking at festivals](/blog/drug-checking-services-festivals.html).

## The realistic default: reagents plus strips

For most people in the US, mailing a sample somewhere is not going to happen before the weekend. The at-home combination is a reagent kit for substance identification and immunoassay strips for fentanyl.

Be clear about what this does and does not buy you. Colour reagents are presumptive identification, not quantification: they suggest which compound family is present and tell you nothing about milligrams, and a fast dark colour change is not evidence of strength. Test strips are also presumptive and have real specificity limits. A laboratory evaluation of BTNX fentanyl test strips across five manufacturing lots reported cross-reactivity with fentanyl and eleven analogues, false positives from diphenhydramine, lidocaine, MDMA and methamphetamine, and notable lot-to-lot sensitivity variation ([PMID 38238757](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38238757/)). The drug supply also changes faster than any single technology tracks, which is the argument for using more than one method ([PMID 37506334](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37506334/)).

Practical setup:

1. **A multi-reagent kit** so you can cross-check one reaction against another. Our [reagent colour chart](/reagent-chart.html) shows the expected reactions, and [which test kit do I need](/which-test-kit.html) matches kits to substances. Kits are available through our [test kit page](/kits) and our [vendor comparison](/test-kits.html).
2. **[Fentanyl test strips](/fentanyl-strips)**, used correctly. Two lines means not detected, one line means detected, which is the single most misread result in harm reduction. See [how to use fentanyl test strips](/blog/how-to-use-fentanyl-test-strips.html).
3. **Test more than one spot** of a batch. Uneven distribution means a clean result on one pinch does not clear the rest.

## The bottom line

DrugsData is not coming back before 2027 on its own account, and the still-working results database is the reason so many people do not realise it. The replacements are real but fragmented: one paid commercial lab, a handful of university and public health programs that you usually reach through a local harm reduction organisation, and event-based checking where it exists. Everything else falls back to reagents and strips at home. Check any service's own site for current status before you post a sample, because the status of every one of these programs has changed at least once in the last two years.
