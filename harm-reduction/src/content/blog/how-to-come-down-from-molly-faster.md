You cannot come down from molly or coke faster in any pharmacological sense. Elimination is set by your liver enzymes and kidneys, and nothing available at 4am changes that rate. What you can change is how the remaining hours feel: agitation, body temperature, blood sugar, and whether you eventually sleep. Cooling, food, measured hydration, and sleep genuinely help. Sweating it out, chugging water, coffee, cold showers, and vitamin C shorten nothing. And occasionally a high that refuses to end is not a high at all. It is a medical emergency wearing the same clothes.

## Quick answers

**Can you make MDMA wear off faster?** No. MDMA's elimination half-life is roughly 7 to 8 hours and its active metabolite MDA runs 10.5 to 12.5 hours ([PMID 18520604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18520604/)). Half-life is a property of your enzymes, not your behavior.

**How do I stop being high right now?** You cannot switch it off. You can reduce the load: leave the stimulating environment, get cool, eat carbohydrates and protein, sit down, stop redosing. Everything else is waiting.

**Does drinking lots of water flush MDMA out?** No, and it can kill you. MDMA lowers serum sodium, and a controlled human study found water loading dropped sodium further after MDMA than after placebo ([PMID 27403159](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27403159/)). Sip to thirst, roughly 500 mL per hour maximum while dancing.

**What do hospitals give for stimulant agitation?** Benzodiazepines, usually IV or IM midazolam or diazepam. They are first-line in the American Heart Association statement on cocaine-associated chest pain ([PMID 18347214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18347214/)). They treat symptoms; they do not clear the drug either.

**When is a comedown an emergency?** Temperature above 39°C / 102°F, muscle rigidity or jerking, chest pain, confusion or paranoia that will not settle, seizure, or very dark urine. Call 911 for any of these.

## Why nothing speeds up elimination

Both drugs are cleared by enzymatic metabolism in the liver, at a rate set by enzyme availability and hepatic blood flow. There is no consumer-accessible lever for it.

**MDMA** is the slow one. Kolbrich et al. tracked plasma levels in 17 volunteers for up to 143 hours after controlled oral doses ([PMID 18520604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18520604/)): MDMA half-life around 7 to 8 hours, MDA 10.5 to 12.5 hours. At the usual five-half-lives rule, that is 35 to 40 hours from a single dose, longer if you redosed.

MDMA's metabolism is also saturable. De la Torre et al. showed that raising the dose produces disproportionately larger plasma concentrations, because MDMA inhibits the CYP2D6 enzyme that clears it ([PMID 10671903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10671903/)). A second pill is not a second unit of drug. It lands on a pathway the first one already partly blocked.

**Cocaine** is the fast one. Plasma half-life is roughly 38 to 39 minutes regardless of dose or route ([PMID 1294836](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1294836/)). That is why people redose compulsively, and why total exposure across a night gets enormous even though each hit fades quickly.

See also [how long MDMA lasts](/blog/how-long-does-mdma-last.html) and [how long MDMA stays in your system](/blog/how-long-does-mdma-stay-in-your-system.html).

## What actually helps in the next few hours

None of this shortens the drug's stay. All of it makes the tail more tolerable and lowers the odds of it turning into something worse.

- **Cooling**: get out of the crowd, into moving air, shade, or air conditioning. Hyperthermia drives most serious MDMA harm, and temperature is one of the few variables you control.
- **Blood sugar and food**: stimulants suppress appetite while you burn glucose dancing, and low blood sugar mimics and worsens anxiety and shakiness. Eat carbohydrates plus protein, not an energy drink.
- **Measured hydration with electrolytes**: replace what you sweat, not more. Plain water in volume is the hyponatremia risk. [Electrolyte packets](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=electrolyte+powder+packets&tag=ravewellness01-20) are the better default after hours of sweating.
- **Stop redosing**: the highest-value decision available. Every redose extends the timeline, and for MDMA it does so nonlinearly.
- **Sleep**: it does not accelerate metabolism, but it removes you from the hours you are trying to escape. Our [post-rave sleep guide](/blog/how-to-sleep-after-a-rave.html) covers melatonin dosing and what to avoid.
- **Reduce stimulation**: dark, quiet, horizontal. Sympathetic arousal feeds on light, noise, and crowds.

For the next-day and next-week picture, a different problem from this one, see our [MDMA comedown guide](/blog/mdma-comedown.html) and [post-festival recovery guide](/blog/post-festival-recovery.html).

## Benzodiazepines: the real medical answer, and the real risks

Benzodiazepines are what emergency departments reach for when someone is dangerously agitated on a stimulant. They reduce sympathetic drive, lower heart rate and blood pressure, and indirectly help with temperature. The 2008 AHA statement makes IV benzodiazepines early management for cocaine-associated chest pain and warns against beta-blockers there ([PMID 18347214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18347214/)). A randomized trial in acute agitation found a midazolam-droperidol combination reached adequate sedation faster than droperidol or olanzapine alone ([PMID 27745766](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27745766/)).

**None of that makes taking a Xanax at home the same intervention.** In hospital, someone is watching your airway, temperature, and rhythm. At home: stimulant-impaired memory makes accidental redosing of the benzo common, any GHB or alcohol on board turns it into a respiratory depression problem, and using one after every night out builds dependence quietly. Timing and tradeoffs are in the [post-rave sleep guide](/blog/how-to-sleep-after-a-rave.html). Check the [interaction checker](/interactions.html) before combining anything.

## What does not work

- **Sweating it out**: sweat is a negligible elimination route, since both drugs are cleared hepatically. Sauna, more dancing, or extra layers add heat load to a body already thermoregulating badly. Not neutral, actively dangerous.
- **Drinking water to flush it**: it does not increase clearance meaningfully and it causes hyponatremia. Documented cases progress from SIADH to water intoxication with seizures and coma ([PMID 30158258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30158258/)).
- **Coffee or energy drinks**: a stimulant on top of a stimulant. The extra adrenergic load raises heart rate and anxiety when you want less of both.
- **Cold showers as a fix**: cooling off is worth something, but it does nothing to the drug. Aggressive cooling treats hyperthermia, not being high.
- **Vitamin C, NAC, and antioxidants**: neuroprotection theories with rodent-only evidence, not acute reversal agents. See our [MDMA supplement protocol review](/blog/mdma-supplements-protocol.html) for what the evidence supports.
- **Activated charcoal after the fact**: charcoal binds drug still in the gut. Position-paper data show absorption reduction falls to clinically questionable levels past about one hour post-ingestion. If you are already high, absorption already happened.
- **Acidifying your urine**: acidic urine does increase amphetamine excretion, but it does not reduce toxicity and it promotes myoglobin precipitation in the kidneys. In anyone with muscle breakdown from a hot night, that is a direct route to acute kidney injury.

## When "not coming down" is an emergency

Call 911 and say what was taken. Responders need the drug name to treat correctly, and most US states have Good Samaritan protections.

- **Hyperthermia**: temperature above 39°C / 102°F, hot dry or drenched skin, confusion. The leading mechanism in MDMA deaths. Aggressive cooling is the treatment; a case series documented ice water immersion dropping core temperature 0.18 to 0.28°C per minute, faster than mist-and-fan ([PMID 25695144](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25695144/)). While waiting for help, cool the person with whatever you have.
- **Serotonin toxicity**: the Hunter criteria are clonus (especially spontaneous or ocular), agitation, sweating, tremor, hyperreflexia, and temperature above 38°C ([PMID 12925718](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12925718/)). Clonus plus fever is what separates this from a hard comedown. Risk rises sharply with an SSRI, MAOI, or tramadol on board. See our [serotonin syndrome guide](/blog/serotonin-syndrome.html).
- **Stimulant psychosis**: paranoia, hallucinations, or delusions persisting after the drug should have faded. Even at prescribed doses, new-onset psychosis occurred in roughly 1 in 660 young people on stimulants in a cohort of 221,846 patients ([PMID 30893533](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30893533/)). Recreational doses plus sleep deprivation raise that substantially.
- **Chest pain**: cocaine-associated chest pain is a coronary event until proven otherwise and can start hours after use. Go to an emergency department and tell them it was cocaine, because the treatment differs ([PMID 18347214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18347214/)).
- **Very dark or cola-colored urine**: a sign of rhabdomyolysis. Needs IV fluids, not more water by mouth.

If the high is lasting dramatically longer than it should, consider that you may not have taken what you were told. Cathinones and methamphetamine sold as MDMA produce much longer, more agitated experiences. A [reagent test kit](/kits) answers that before the night instead of during it.

## The honest summary

Time is the only thing that clears MDMA or cocaine. Everything sold as an accelerant is useless or harmful, and the two most popular myths, sweating it out and drinking water to flush it, are the two most likely to put you in a hospital. Cool down, eat, sip electrolytes to thirst, stop redosing, and let sleep take the rest.
