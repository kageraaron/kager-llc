Liebermann is a confirmation reagent, not a first reagent. **It runs light yellow to orange with cocaine, brown-black to black with MDMA, and a very pale yellow with ketamine, and its main job is to add a second data point to a result you already got from Marquis or Morris.** It is also the reagent people reach for when they want information about cocaine adulterants. This is the color chart, the 40-second reading window, and the specific claims about Liebermann that have changed as the drug supply changed.

## Quick answers

**What color is cocaine on Liebermann?** A range: light yellow, through orange, to a brick red-orange. All of those are within the expected range for cocaine.

**What color is MDMA on Liebermann?** An intense brown-black to black, and it forms fast.

**How long do you have to read it?** About 40 to 45 seconds. Liebermann is one of the fast reagents. Every reagent eventually darkens on its own, so a color that only appears after several minutes is not a result.

**Does Liebermann detect levamisole in cocaine?** Not directly, and the guidance has been revised. Per DanceSafe, a light yellow reaction means levamisole is not present, while an orange reaction means levamisole might be present, because cocaine sometimes reads orange without any levamisole at all.

## The Liebermann color chart

Liebermann is potassium nitrite in concentrated sulfuric acid, and it starts out clear and colorless. Read it fast.

| Substance | Liebermann color |
|-----------|------------------|
| **Cocaine** | **Light yellow → orange → brick red-orange** |
| **MDMA and MDA** | **Intense brown-black to black**, fast |
| PMA and PMMA | Purple-brown |
| Amphetamine and methamphetamine | Orange to red |
| **Ketamine** | **Very pale yellow**, sometimes hard to see |
| 2C-B | Light yellow through dark black with a yellow ring |
| Heroin and morphine | Purple to black |
| Oxycodone | No reaction → faint brown → reddish-purplish-brown |
| Cathinones (mephedrone, methcathinone) | Bright yellow |
| Methylone | Orange → brown |
| MDPV | Yellow → green |
| 4-FA | Orange to reddish-orange |
| DMT | Brown → black |
| MXE | Orange-brown |
| 3-MeO-PCP | Dark reddish brown |
| Acetaminophen (paracetamol) | Purple-brown |
| Alprazolam | No reaction |
| LSD | No fast, vivid reaction |

## The one reaction worth memorizing

**MDMA goes black quickly. PMA and PMMA go purple-brown.** That difference matters more than any other line on the chart. PMA and PMMA are sold as ecstasy, act far more slowly so people redose, and interfere with the body's ability to shed heat, which is why they turn up in fatal overheating cases. A sample sold as molly that reads purple-brown rather than black on Liebermann is a serious warning.

The catch is that acetaminophen also reads purple-brown, so Liebermann alone cannot separate a PMMA sample from a paracetamol-cut sample. That is exactly the situation multiple reagents exist for. Run Marquis and Simon's as well, and read the [Marquis color guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-marquis-reagent-result.html) so you can interpret them together.

## Cocaine, orange, and what changed

Liebermann is the second step in DanceSafe's cocaine kit, after Morris. The older harm reduction folklore was that an orange Liebermann meant levamisole. That is no longer how the reaction is read.

The current guidance is narrower and more honest: a **light yellow** result means levamisole is not there, while an **orange** result means levamisole may or may not be there, because cocaine on its own now produces orange reactions with some frequency. DanceSafe revised this publicly as the supply shifted.

So use Liebermann on cocaine as a range check rather than an adulterant test. Anything from light yellow to orange fits cocaine. What Liebermann cannot do is tell you what your cocaine is cut with, and it cannot see fentanyl. Cocaine is now among the most common non-opioid drugs found contaminated with fentanyl, so run a strip every time. ## How to run the test correctly

1. **Use a pinhead-sized sample.** Roughly 10 mg, finely crushed. More sample makes the color harder to read, not easier.
2. **Put it on a white ceramic plate** or the kit's test tray, never on metal or a surface the acid can eat.
3. **Add one drop** without letting the dropper touch the sample.
4. **Read within the first 40 to 45 seconds** and note both the color and how fast it developed. Speed is information.
5. **Use a blank**, a drop of reagent by itself, to compare against. Reagents are tinted and darken with age, so a blank prevents you from reading the bottle's own color as a reaction.
6. **Read in daylight or under a white LED.** Colored lighting is the top cause of misjudged reagent colors.

## The mistakes that cause misreads

- **Reading late.** A brown or black color that shows up at three minutes is oxidation, not a result.
- **Overloading the plate.** A large pile can look uniformly black and hide the actual hue, which matters when you are trying to distinguish black from purple-brown.
- **Treating pale yellow as "nothing."** With ketamine the expected reaction genuinely is very faint yellow. Compare against your blank before you call it a non-reaction.
- **Using Liebermann as a standalone ID.** It is a confirmation reagent. Cocaine, cathinones, and several other things all live in the yellow-to-orange band.
- **Assuming a mixture will show up.** Darker colors overshadow lighter ones, and a small amount of a second substance may produce no visible change.

## What Liebermann cannot tell you

It cannot measure purity or dose. There is no at-home reagent that quantifies anything, so a strong color says nothing about how much active drug is in the sample. It cannot detect fentanyl or benzodiazepine adulterants. And it cannot confirm that only one substance is present.

Liebermann is also not the right tool for blotter. Reading an indole means using Ehrlich, covered in our [Ehrlich reagent color chart](/blog/ehrlich-reagent-color-chart.html), and the broader case for testing acid is in [should you test LSD](/blog/should-you-test-lsd.html).

## The bottom line

Read Liebermann inside 45 seconds against a blank, under white light: **yellow to orange is the cocaine range, fast brown-black is MDMA, purple-brown is a PMA/PMMA warning, and very pale yellow fits ketamine.** Pair it with Marquis, Morris, or Simon's depending on what you are checking, add a fentanyl strip regardless, and remember that no color tells you dose.
