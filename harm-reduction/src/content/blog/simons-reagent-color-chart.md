Simon's reagent is the one test that separates MDMA from MDA, and it is the reason a single-reagent kit is never enough. **MDMA turns Simon's blue. MDA either does not react at all or turns a muddy gray-green.** Methamphetamine also turns blue and amphetamine does not, which makes Simon's the standard way to tell those two apart as well. What Simon's cannot do is confirm what you have on its own, measure dose or purity, or detect fentanyl. This guide is the color chart, the chemistry that explains it, and the mistakes that cause misreads.

## Quick answers

**What color is MDMA on a Simon's test?** Blue. A clear, definite blue is the expected reaction for MDMA.

**What does MDA look like on Simon's?** Two results are both expected. MDA may produce no reaction, or it may turn a muddy gray-green to blackish color. DanceSafe added the second reaction after it started showing up around 2021 and lists both as expected for an MDA-like substance.

**Does a blue Simon's result prove I have MDMA?** No. Methamphetamine, mephedrone, PMMA, and MAPB compounds also turn Simon's blue. Blue means "a secondary amine is present," and you need [Marquis](/blog/how-to-read-a-marquis-reagent-result.html) alongside it to say which one.

**Why does Simon's come in two bottles?** Simon's is a two-part reagent. Bottle A and bottle B both go onto the same sample. Using A and B on two different samples is one of the most common errors people make.

**Can Simon's detect fentanyl?** No. No reagent detects fentanyl. Use a [fentanyl test strip](/blog/how-to-use-fentanyl-test-strips.html) every time, regardless of what the colors show.

**Where do you get Simon's?** The [DanceSafe Simon's reagent kit](/simons-kit) is sold on its own, and Simon's A and B are both included in the [MDMA testing kit](/mdma-kit) along with Marquis and Froehde.

## The Simon's color chart

Simon's reads fast. Put both drops on the same sample and judge the color inside the first 45 to 60 seconds, before the natural drift that every reagent shows.

| Substance | Simon's color |
|-----------|---------------|
| **MDMA** | **Blue** |
| **MDA** | **No reaction, or a muddy gray-green** (both are expected) |
| Methamphetamine | Blue |
| Amphetamine | No reaction |
| Mephedrone (4-MMC) | Blue |
| PMMA | Blue |
| 5-MAPB / 6-MAPB | Blue |
| 5-APB / 6-APB | No reaction |
| Cocaine | No reaction |
| Ketamine | No reaction |
| Heroin | No reaction |
| LSD | No reaction |
| Mescaline | No reaction |
| 2C-I | No reaction |
| Sugars / inert fillers | No reaction |
| 2C-B | Inconsistent, not readable |
| Oxycodone | Not enough data to publish a color |

Colors follow DanceSafe's Expected Reactions Color Chart, version 17. The headline: **blue means a secondary amine, and no reaction means a primary amine or something Simon's ignores entirely.**

## Why blue happens, and why it is not proof

Simon's is two solutions. Bottle A is sodium nitroprusside with acetaldehyde in water, bottle B is sodium carbonate in water. Together they detect **secondary amines**, and the blue color is the signal that one is present.

That single fact explains the whole chart. MDMA carries an N-methyl group on its nitrogen, making it a secondary amine, so it goes blue. MDA lacks that methyl group, making it a primary amine, so it does not. The same difference sits between methamphetamine, which is a secondary amine and goes blue, and amphetamine, which does not.

The catch is that "secondary amine" is a category, not an identity. Methamphetamine, mephedrone, PMMA, and the MAPB compounds are all secondary amines and all turn blue. So a blue Simon's on its own does not tell you that you have MDMA. It tells you that whatever is dominant in your sample has that structural feature.

This is why the reagents are read as a set, across a single row:

- Marquis purple-to-black **plus** Simon's blue is consistent with MDMA.
- Marquis orange-to-brown **plus** Simon's blue is consistent with methamphetamine, not MDMA.
- Marquis purple-to-black **plus** Simon's no reaction or gray-green is consistent with MDA.
- No reaction on Marquis **plus** blue on Simon's is a red flag pointing toward something like a cathinone.

If your reactions do not all line up on one row of the chart, the result is "unexpected" and the sample should be treated as unknown.

## Why the MDMA versus MDA answer matters

MDA is not a milder version of MDMA. It runs longer, roughly 8 to 12 hours against 4 to 6 for MDMA, it is more visual and hallucinogenic, and animal research points to greater neurotoxic potential at comparable doses. Someone who redoses on the assumption they took MDMA can end up far deeper into a much longer experience than they planned. The full comparison is in [MDA vs MDMA](/blog/mda-vs-mdma.html).

Marquis cannot make this call. MDMA and MDA both turn Marquis purple to black, and both go near-black on Mecke and Froehde. Simon's is the only reagent in a standard kit that resolves it, which is why every serious MDMA workflow includes it.

## How to run Simon's correctly

1. **Use a fresh sample.** If you already ran Marquis, do not add Simon's to that spent reaction. Crush a new pinhead-sized grain onto a clean white ceramic or hard plastic surface.
2. **Add one drop of bottle A, then one drop of bottle B, to the same sample.** Both parts, one sample. Do not touch either dropper to the sample, and never put a cap back on the wrong bottle.
3. **Read within the first minute** under white daylight or a white LED, not a phone screen or colored bar lighting.
4. **Run a blank.** A drop of A plus B by itself, next to your test, shows you the reagent's own color so you do not mistake it for a reaction.
5. **Compare against Marquis on a separate sample** before drawing any conclusion. Simon's alone answers only half the question.
6. **Clean up.** Neutralize with baking soda, then wash with soap and water and dry thoroughly before the next test.

## The mistakes that cause misreads

- **Splitting A and B across two samples.** They must go on the same grain. This is the single most common Simon's error.
- **Calling blue a confirmation.** Blue narrows the field to secondary amines. It does not name the molecule.
- **Expecting MDA to always do nothing.** Since 2021 the muddy gray-green result has been just as expected. A dark murky reaction is not automatically a contaminated test.
- **Reading a pressed pill without accounting for dye.** Colored binders can tint a reaction and make a weak color unreadable. Test more than one sample when a pill is heavily dyed.
- **Reading too late or in bad light.** Both push a real result out of the range shown on the chart.
- **Assuming blue means safe.** A blue reaction says nothing about dose, nothing about what else is in the sample, and nothing about fentanyl.

## The bottom line

Simon's answers one question extremely well: is there a secondary amine in this sample? **Blue means yes, which is consistent with MDMA or methamphetamine depending on what Marquis showed. No reaction or a muddy gray-green means MDA or amphetamine.** Read it inside the first minute, run both bottles on one sample, and never read it in isolation.

Reagent tests identify the presence of a substance class. They do not establish purity, they do not establish dose, they cannot tell you whether more than one drug is present, and they cannot rule out fentanyl. Only a fentanyl test strip or a laboratory can do that last part.
