R and S ketamine are real, distinct molecules with different pharmacology, and **you cannot tell which one you have.** Outside a pharmaceutical supply chain, ketamine is **racemic**: a 50/50 mixture of the two mirror-image forms, (R)-ketamine (arketamine) and (S)-ketamine (esketamine). S-ketamine binds the NMDA receptor several times more tightly and is the enantiomer in Spravato. R-ketamine is the weaker NMDA blocker, yet rodent studies suggest it may be the *better* antidepressant with fewer dissociative and abuse-related effects. That is the genuinely interesting part of the story. What no reagent kit, fentanyl strip or at-home method can do is distinguish them, because chirality is invisible to a colour reaction. Anyone selling "R-ketamine" recreationally cannot back the claim up, and neither can you check it.

## Quick answers

**Is street ketamine R or S?** Effectively always both. Illicit synthesis yields the racemate, and separating enantiomers is a deliberate extra lab step with no street incentive to perform it.

**Can a reagent test tell R from S ketamine?** No. Morris and Mandelin reagents detect ketamine as a substance class. Enantiomers have identical connectivity and produce identical colour reactions.

**How would you actually distinguish them?** Chiral chromatography in a laboratory, using HPLC, GC, SFC or capillary electrophoresis with a chiral selector ([PMID 32540082](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32540082/)). Nothing at home approximates this.

**Is R-ketamine safer?** Preclinically it looks that way, with less dissociation and less abuse-related behaviour in animals. Human data is very early and one placebo-controlled pilot was negative.

**Does the texture tell you whether it is R or S?** No. Crystal size and texture come from how a batch was recrystallised and handled, not from chirality. Finer powder does pack more drug into the same visual line, which is a real dosing risk.

**Does the enantiomer change bladder or cognitive risk?** Not in any way you can act on. Those harms track cumulative exposure and frequency.

## What "R" and "S" actually mean

Ketamine has one chiral centre, a carbon atom with four different groups attached. That gives two non-superimposable mirror-image versions, called enantiomers. Same formula, same molecular weight, same melting point, same solubility. What differs is three-dimensional shape, and shape is what protein binding sites read.

Standard ketamine, veterinary and human anaesthetic and recreational alike, is **(R,S)-ketamine**, an equal mixture of both. Isolating either one takes a deliberate resolution or asymmetric synthesis step.

## S-ketamine (esketamine): the potent one

S-ketamine is the more potent of the two at the NMDA receptor, which is the target most of ketamine's dissociative and anaesthetic effects run through. Quantified in clinical terms, esketamine delivers about **three times the analgesic potency and 1.5 times the anaesthetic potency** of arketamine ([PMID 39457596](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39457596/), Kawczak 2024, *Biomedicines*). For the underlying receptor pharmacology, the standard reference is Zanos 2018 in *Pharmacol Rev* ([PMID 29945898](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29945898/)). Relative to the racemate, it also gives comparable analgesia with less drowsiness ([PMID 38295061](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38295061/), Mion 2024, *Anesth Analg*).

That potency is why esketamine is the enantiomer that got developed and approved, and it is the drug in Spravato. The distinction between Spravato, compounded telehealth ketamine and homemade sprays is covered in our post on [ketamine nasal spray](/blog/ketamine-nasal-spray.html).

## R-ketamine (arketamine): the counterintuitive one

Here is where the story stops being predictable. If antidepressant action came straight from NMDA blockade, the weaker binder should be the weaker antidepressant. **Preclinical work says the reverse.** The 2022 review by Zhang in *Neuropharmacology* states it directly: a growing body of preclinical studies show arketamine has **greater potency and longer-lasting antidepressant-like effects than esketamine in rodents, despite its lower NMDA receptor binding affinity**, with less psychotomimetic effect, less dissociation and lower abuse liability ([PMID 35977629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35977629/)).

Why would that happen? The leading explanation is that ketamine's antidepressant action is not simply NMDA channel block. It runs through downstream events, including metabolite activity and synaptic plasticity signalling, that do not track receptor binding affinity. Dissociation, psychotomimetic effects and abuse-related reinforcement *do* track NMDA potency closely. If those mechanisms come apart, the weaker NMDA binder can keep the mood effect while shedding the side effects. That is the hypothesis arketamine development is built on ([PMID 29945898](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29945898/)).

**Now the honesty part.** Most of that advantage is rodent data. The human evidence is thin and mixed:

- An **open-label pilot** of intravenous arketamine (0.5 mg/kg) in 7 people with treatment-resistant depression reported MADRS scores falling from 30.7 to 10.4 at one day, with dissociation nearly absent ([PMID 32078034](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32078034/), Leal 2021). Seven people, no control group, no blinding.
- The follow-up **randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover pilot** in 10 patients found a main effect of time but **no significant effect of treatment**. Arketamine was safe and well tolerated, but it did not beat saline in that trial ([PMID 36871913](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36871913/), Leal 2023, *J Affect Disord*).

A cross-sectional analysis of registered protocols confirms the picture: arketamine trials remain far smaller and earlier in development than the esketamine programme ([PMID 40440859](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40440859/), Swieczkowski 2025, *Psychiatry Res*). Arketamine is a promising research compound, not an established better ketamine, and nobody should present it as one.

## Can you tell what you have? No.

**Recreational ketamine is essentially always racemic.** Illicit synthesis routes produce both enantiomers in equal amounts. Getting a single one means a resolution step or asymmetric synthesis, extra equipment, and losing half your yield. There is no market pressure to do that when buyers cannot verify the result. A review of chiral analysis in seized and internet-purchased solid samples found that these compound classes are overwhelmingly traded as racemic mixtures ([PMID 32540082](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32540082/), Schmid 2020, *J Chromatogr A*).

**Reagent tests cannot see chirality.** Morris and Mandelin work by reacting with a functional group and producing a colour. Both enantiomers have identical functional groups, so both give identical colours. The reagent tells you the sample behaves like ketamine and nothing about R versus S. Our [reagent colour chart](/reagent-chart.html) covers what those reactions do and do not establish. Fentanyl test strips are antibody-based and equally blind here.

**Distinguishing enantiomers requires chiral chromatography**, meaning a lab instrument with a chiral stationary phase or selector, not a spot plate. The same review lists the techniques used in practice: HPLC, GC, supercritical fluid chromatography, capillary electrophoresis and CEC ([PMID 32540082](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32540082/)).

So: **if someone offers you "pure R-ketamine," they cannot substantiate it and you cannot check it.** Treat the claim as a sales pitch. A [ketamine testing kit](/ketamine-kit) is still worth using, because the real risk is that your powder is not ketamine at all, but be clear on what it buys you: **it confirms the substance is ketamine, and tells you nothing whatsoever about which enantiomer.** Testing kits are from DanceSafe, a nonprofit.

## Shards versus powder: what texture does and does not tell you

"Big shards mean it is the good stuff" is one of the most persistent beliefs about ketamine, and it is wrong in three separate ways.

**Texture tells you nothing about the enantiomer.** Crystal habit comes from how the product was recrystallised: which solvent, how fast it cooled, how quickly it evaporated. After that it depends on handling and storage, since ketamine HCl clumps and cakes with humidity, and on whether anyone crushed it along the way. None of those variables involves chirality. Two batches of chemically identical racemic ketamine can look completely different, and street ketamine is racemic regardless.

**Texture is a poor purity signal too.** Crystals can be cut, and a pure product can arrive as fine powder because of how it was processed or because it got crushed in transit. Appearance is not a purity test, and nothing consumer-accessible measures purity or potency.

**The part that actually matters: texture changes volume, not potency per milligram.** A milligram of ketamine is a milligram no matter what it looks like. But finely ground powder packs far more densely than coarse shards, so **the same visually sized line holds meaningfully more drug when the material is finer**. Someone who eyeballs their usual-looking line after switching from shards to a fine powder can take substantially more than they intended, with nothing visible to warn them. That is the ordinary explanation for "this batch hit way harder," far more often than anything about the drug itself.

Which is the argument for weighing rather than eyeballing. A [milligram scale](/mg-scale) removes the variable entirely, since 40 mg is 40 mg whether it started as shards or dust. DanceSafe, nonprofit.

## The risks that actually matter do not care about chirality

Even if you could verify enantiomer content, it would not change the harm profile. The dose-dependent harms with the strongest evidence scale with **how much you use and how often**, not with stereochemistry:

- **Bladder damage**: ketamine-induced uropathy tracks cumulative exposure. See [ketamine bladder damage](/blog/ketamine-bladder-damage.html).
- **Cognitive effects**: memory and executive function deficits show up in heavy, frequent users. See [does ketamine cause brain damage](/blog/does-ketamine-cause-brain-damage.html).
- **Dependence**: compulsive redosing is driven by the drug's short duration and tolerance build, not by isomer ratio. See [is ketamine addictive](/blog/is-ketamine-addictive.html).

The enantiomer question is genuinely interesting pharmacology and completely irrelevant to your frequency limits.

R and S ketamine really do differ, S is the potent NMDA blocker and R is the surprising preclinical antidepressant candidate, but what you buy is racemic, no consumer test can distinguish them, and any "R-ketamine" claim on the recreational market is unverifiable by design.
