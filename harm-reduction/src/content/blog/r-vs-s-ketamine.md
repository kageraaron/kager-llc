**R vs S ketamine is mainly a difference in strength, not in the kind of high, and you cannot tell which one you have.** Street ketamine is racemic: a 50/50 mix of two mirror-image molecules, (S)-ketamine (esketamine) and (R)-ketamine (arketamine). S is the stronger one, roughly 1.5 to 2 times as strong as racemic ketamine by weight, and R is the weakest. At matched strength, the only controlled human comparison found S and racemic ketamine felt no different.

The practical risk is not picking the "wrong" form. It is taking your usual amount of a form that is stronger than you think.

## Quick answers

**Is S-ketamine stronger than regular ketamine?** Yes. Anaesthesia practice gives about half the racemic dose, though animal data suggest the true ratio may be closer to 1.5 than 2.

**Does R-ketamine give a gentler high?** Not in any way that has been shown in people. In studies it mostly does less, because it is a weaker drug.

**Can a test kit tell R, S and racemic ketamine apart?** No. Reagents and fentanyl strips cannot see the difference. Only chiral lab analysis can.

**Which one is best for recreational use?** None is safer. Bladder damage, dependence and dangerous mixes apply to all three, and switching forms without adjusting the amount is the biggest risk.

## What R, S and racemic mean

Ketamine has one chiral centre: a carbon with four different groups on it. That produces two non-superimposable mirror images, called enantiomers. Same formula, same molecular weight, same chemistry in a test tube. The only difference is three-dimensional shape, and shape is exactly what a receptor reads.

Standard ketamine, veterinary, human anaesthetic and recreational alike, is (R,S)-ketamine, an equal mixture. Pure S-ketamine is a prescription drug: the Spravato nasal spray for depression, and an anaesthetic in parts of Europe. Pure R-ketamine is not approved anywhere and turns up online as a "research chemical". The differences between Spravato, compounded telehealth ketamine and homemade sprays are covered in the [ketamine nasal spray](/blog/ketamine-nasal-spray.html) post.

## How much stronger is S-ketamine?

S binds the NMDA receptor several times more tightly than R, and NMDA blockade drives most of ketamine's dissociative and anaesthetic effects. Kawczak's 2024 review in *Biomedicines* puts esketamine at roughly **three times the analgesic potency and 1.5 times the anaesthetic potency** of arketamine. Zanos' 2018 review in *Pharmacological Reviews* is the standard reference for the receptor pharmacology.

Against racemic ketamine, the clinical rule of thumb is that S is twice as strong, which is why anaesthetists give it at about half the racemic dose. The controlled evidence is softer. A study in dogs found S-ketamine only about 1.5 times as potent as racemic ketamine for induction, and roughly equal when infused ([PMID 25041216](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25041216/)). That is animal data at anaesthetic doses. **The safe reading is that S is somewhere between 1.5 and 2 times as strong as racemic, and you will not know where a given batch sits.**

## Do they feel different?

Online you will find confident claims that S is "harsher", racemic is "more euphoric" and R is "gentle". Very little human research has tested this, and what exists points to dose, not form.

**S vs racemic, matched for strength.** A randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial gave 30 healthy men an IV infusion of S-ketamine, racemic ketamine at an equivalent strength, or placebo ([PMID 33487513](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33487513/)). Both drugs produced clear psychological effects and cognitive impairment compared with placebo, and **there was no significant difference between S and racemic ketamine**. The authors noted this contradicted the older idea that S causes fewer unpleasant mental effects. It was small and given by IV, but it is the best comparison there is.

**R's "gentle" reputation.** It traces mostly to a 1997 brain-imaging study in healthy volunteers ([PMID 9088882](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9088882/)). S-ketamine produced ego dissolution and hallucination-like effects, while the same number of molecules of R produced relaxation and no psychotic-like symptoms. Because R binds the receptor several times more weakly, an equal-weight dose of R is a much smaller effective dose. "Milder" may simply mean "weaker".

**S is not less recreational.** Before Spravato was approved, regulators required an abuse-potential study in 34 experienced recreational drug users ([NCT02682225](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02682225)). It compared nasal esketamine at 84 mg and 112 mg with IV racemic ketamine at 0.5 mg/kg. All three were rated as more "liked" than placebo, and the 112 mg dose scored highest for feeling "detached", "floating" and "spaced out", according to the [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/211243s019lbl.pdf).

## R is the counterintuitive one

If antidepressant action came straight from NMDA blockade, the weaker binder should be the weaker antidepressant. Rodent studies say the reverse. Zhang's 2022 review in *Neuropharmacology* describes arketamine as having **greater and longer-lasting antidepressant-like effects than esketamine in rodents, despite lower NMDA receptor binding**, with less dissociation and lower abuse liability. The leading explanation is that the mood effect runs through downstream plasticity signalling that does not track binding strength, while dissociation and reward do.

The human evidence is thin. Leal's 2021 open-label pilot gave IV arketamine to 7 people with treatment-resistant depression and reported a large drop in depression scores with dissociation nearly absent ([PMID 32078034](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32078034/)). A randomised, placebo-controlled crossover pilot in 10 patients then found **no significant effect of treatment** ([PMID 36871913](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36871913/)). The largest trial, 102 patients given 30 mg or 60 mg IV, missed its main goal, with dissociation and sedation at placebo-like rates. That result comes from the [sponsor's announcement](https://ir.atai.com/news-releases/news-release-details/atai-life-sciences-announces-results-phase-2a-trial-pcn-101-r) and has not been peer-reviewed.

## Can you tell what you have? No

Recreational ketamine is racemic, near enough always. Making a single enantiomer takes a resolution step or asymmetric synthesis and costs half the yield, and buyers cannot verify the result anyway. Schmid's 2020 review of chiral analysis in seized and internet-purchased samples found these compounds overwhelmingly traded as racemates. A 2025 Chinese study of ketamine users' hair found R-to-S ratios close to the 1:1 of a racemate, with pure S only where someone had used pharmaceutical esketamine ([doi:10.1016/j.jpba.2025.116824](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpba.2025.116824)).

Reagent tests cannot see chirality. Morris and Mandelin react with a functional group, and both enantiomers have identical functional groups, so both give identical colours. Fentanyl strips are antibody-based and equally blind here. The [reagent colour chart](/reagent-chart.html) covers what those reactions do and do not establish. Separating enantiomers takes chiral chromatography or capillary electrophoresis in a lab. Nothing on a spot plate approximates it.

So if someone offers you "pure R" or "pure S" ketamine, they cannot substantiate it and you cannot check it.

## Shards versus powder

"Big shards mean it's the good stuff" is wrong three ways. **Texture says nothing about the enantiomer**: crystal habit comes from the solvent, cooling speed and handling, not chirality. **Texture is a poor purity signal**: crystals can be cut, and pure product can arrive as dust. **Texture changes volume, not potency**: finely ground powder packs far more densely than shards, so **the same visually sized line holds meaningfully more drug when the material is finer**. That is the usual explanation for "this batch hit way harder". A [milligram scale](/mg-scale) removes the variable, because the scale reads the same whether it started as shards or powder.

## Is one better for recreation?

Not in any way the evidence supports, and the question hides the real risk.

- **S-ketamine**: your usual amount becomes a bigger dose. If a batch is new, or sold as S or esketamine, start with a small amount, wait the full onset and don't redose quickly.
- **R-ketamine**: if it "isn't doing much", redosing mostly adds bladder exposure for little effect.
- **Racemic**: the reference point, with every ketamine harm attached.

The harms with the best evidence scale with how much and how often, not with stereochemistry. **Bladder damage** tracks cumulative exposure, and nobody has compared the enantiomers; see [ketamine bladder damage](/blog/ketamine-bladder-damage.html). **Dependence** comes from the short duration and fast tolerance; see [is ketamine addictive](/blog/is-ketamine-addictive.html). **Depressants** such as alcohol, GHB and benzodiazepines are dangerous with any form; see [ketamine and alcohol](/blog/ketamine-and-alcohol.html).

S is stronger, R is weaker, and street ketamine is a mixture of both that no consumer test can separate. Treat any new batch as the strongest form. For k-hole safety and bladder protection, see the [ketamine guide](/ketamine.html).
