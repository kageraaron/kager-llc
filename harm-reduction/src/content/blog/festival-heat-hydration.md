**Overheating, not overdose in the usual sense, is what kills people who die acutely from MDMA.** A 2004 review in the *European Journal of Pharmacology* opens on exactly that point: hyperthermia is the predominant severe acute adverse effect following recreational MDMA use.

So staying cool at a festival is not a comfort issue. On a hot dance floor it is the single most useful thing you can do for yourself. The complication is that the obvious fix, drinking a lot of water, has killed people too.

## Why MDMA makes heat dangerous

Three mechanisms run at once. MDMA drives serotonin and norepinephrine release, which raises metabolic activity and muscle tension, so you produce more heat. It causes cutaneous vasoconstriction, narrowing the blood vessels near the skin, so you shed less of it. And serotonin release in the hypothalamus raises the temperature set-point, meaning the thermostat itself has been turned up.

In a cool room those effects add up to a modest rise. On a packed dance floor with six hours of exertion, the same three mechanisms can push core temperature into organ-damage territory. What follows is disseminated intravascular coagulation, rhabdomyolysis flooding the kidneys with broken-down muscle, liver failure. Going from hot to critical can take under an hour.

**The warning sign that matters most: sweating stops.** Someone hot who has stopped sweating has lost their cooling system. That is the moment to act, not the moment to keep an eye on it.

## The water trap

Harm reduction messaging has focused on dehydration for years. Overhydration is the risk people do not hear about, and it has a body count at festivals.

MDMA triggers release of vasopressin and oxytocin, and both tell your kidneys to hold onto water. A 2024 secondary analysis of four randomized clinical trials in *JAMA Network Open* found that 37% of participants with unrestricted fluid intake developed hyponatremia, against zero cases in the group whose intake was restricted. Oxytocin came out as the main driver. Separate work found MDMA amplifies the sodium-lowering effect of any given volume of water compared with drinking the same amount sober. The same pint is not the same pint.

What that looks like clinically: a 2002 case series documented 17 patients aged 15 to 26 with blood sodium as low as 107 mmol/L after MDMA, against a normal range of 135 to 145. They had hyponatremic encephalopathy, meaning brain swelling from the sodium imbalance, presenting as confusion, seizures, and coma. Several needed intensive care.

So the rule is drink to thirst, not to a schedule:

- **Dancing and sweating hard: about 500 ml, one pint, per hour as an upper limit.** This matches DanceSafe and Release UK guidance.
- **Resting or in a cooler area: around 250 ml per hour.**
- **Do not pre-load large volumes** before going in, and do not make high-sugar sports drinks your main fluid.

Electrolytes matter because sweat takes sodium, potassium, and magnesium with it, and topping up with plain water dilutes what is left. A low-sugar mix with real sodium content, somewhere in the 500 to 1000 mg per serving range, suits heavy sweating better than a sports drink does. [Electrolyte packets](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=LMNT+electrolyte+packets&tag=ravewellness01-20) are the easy version of this to carry.

## Staying cool, practically

**Rest breaks are the intervention.** Every 30 to 45 minutes of dancing, take 10 to 15 minutes off the floor. Shade, sitting down, heart rate coming back. Nothing else on this list does as much.

**Find the cool spaces before you need them.** Most large festivals have chill-out areas or shaded structures. Locate them while you are still thinking clearly.

**Wet skin is your best tool.** Evaporation pulls heat off you faster than anything you can drink. Wet your wrists, the back of your neck, your face. A [handheld misting fan](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=handheld+misting+fan+portable&tag=ravewellness01-20) puts a fine mist straight onto skin and is the most effective single item to have on the floor; a [cooling towel](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=frogg+toggs+chilly+pad+cooling+towel&tag=ravewellness01-20) around the neck holds 30 to 60 minutes before it needs re-wetting.

**Cover up from the sun, not from the air.** Loose, light-coloured, breathable fabric. A [wide-brim hat](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=wide+brim+sun+hat+festival&tag=ravewellness01-20) in straw or cotton cuts the radiant load on an outdoor stage. A [pashmina](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=pashmina+shawl+lightweight&tag=ravewellness01-20) is the item people underrate: shade over the shoulders in the afternoon, and warmth at 3 AM when the temperature drops and MDMA's effect on thermoregulation leaves you suddenly freezing.

**Carry your water rather than queueing for it.** A hydration pack keeps your hands free, and the bite valve encourages small frequent sips instead of gulping a litre at the water station, which is the behaviour that gets people into trouble. A 1.5 to 2 L reservoir covers a long session. DanceSafe sells [hydration packs](/hydration-pack) and is a nonprofit that runs free drug checking at events; [CamelBak and similar](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=camelbak+hydration+pack+2L&tag=ravewellness01-20) work the same way.

**Tell someone how you feel.** Heat deterioration is not always obvious from the inside, and the person who notices you are slurring will usually not be you.

## When it becomes an emergency

Early signs: flushed skin, sweating stopping despite the heat, confusion or behaviour that seems off, muscle cramps, headache, nausea. Above 39°C (102°F) you are in medical territory. Above 40°C (104°F) is life-threatening, and core temperature that high damages liver, kidneys, and brain within minutes.

If someone looks like this:

1. Get them to the coolest place available, shade, a chill-out room, anywhere with air conditioning.
2. Take off excess clothing.
3. Wet them down and get air moving over the skin. Neck, wrists, armpits, groin, where blood vessels run close to the surface.
4. Small sips of water with electrolytes, only if they are conscious and swallowing normally.
5. **Get medics involved now.** Most large festivals have them on site. Do not wait to see whether the person comes around.

How fast they are cooled is the main thing that determines how this ends.
