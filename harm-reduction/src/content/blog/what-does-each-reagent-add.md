**Most people do not need nine reagents. They need one or two, and the rest is coverage they will never use.** Each reagent answers a different question, which is why running two on the same sample is not redundant, but it also means the right number for you depends entirely on how many different substances you test. If you test one thing, a single kit runs 50 to 75 tests and the complete set is coverage you are paying for and not using. Here is what each bottle actually contributes, so you can buy the smallest set that answers your question.

## Quick answers

**Do I need all nine reagents?** Almost certainly not. Nine makes sense if you test across several different substance classes, or if you are supplying a group. For one substance, one kit is the answer.

**Are two reagents on one sample redundant?** No, and this is the thing most people get wrong. Marquis screens for a class; Simon's separates MDMA from MDA within that class. They answer different questions, so a full workup consumes one test's worth from each bottle.

**Which single reagent is most useful?** Marquis, if you only ever buy one. It reacts with the widest range of substances and is the standard first step. It is also the least conclusive on its own.

**What does no reaction mean?** Sometimes it is the answer. On Ehrlich, no colour change on blotter means it is not LSD, because NBOMe compounds are not indoles and do not react at all. A blank result is data.

## The screening reagents

**Marquis** is the broad first step and the one to buy if you buy only one. It reacts across a wide range of substances, which makes it the standard opening move and also means a Marquis result alone rarely settles anything. It tells you which direction to look, then you confirm with something else. Full colour reference in [how to read a Marquis reagent result](/blog/how-to-read-a-marquis-reagent-result.html), or buy it alone as the [Marquis kit](/marquis-kit).

**Mecke** earns its place by separating the methylenedioxy compounds from the amphetamine-type stimulants in a single glance, which is a distinction Marquis blurs. If your concern is whether a sample is MDMA-class or something in the amphetamine family, Mecke answers that faster than anything else. Colours in the [Mecke colour chart](/blog/mecke-reagent-color-chart.html), or the [Mecke kit](/mecke-kit) on its own. Worth noting: Mecke is **not** in the DanceSafe MDMA kit, so it is a separate purchase if you want it.

## The discriminators

These are the reagents that answer a specific question nothing else can, and they are usually the reason to buy a second bottle.

**Simon's** is the MDMA versus MDA discriminator and the single most useful reagent in the MDMA kit. It is a two-part reagent, Simon's A then Simon's B on the same sample. Marquis cannot separate MDMA from MDA because both react similarly, and the distinction matters because MDA is stronger, longer and harder on the body. See the [Simon's colour chart](/blog/simons-reagent-color-chart.html) or the [Simon's kit](/simons-kit).

**Ehrlich** detects indoles, which covers LSD, DMT and psilocybin as a class. Its most valuable result is a negative one: **NBOMe compounds are not indoles and produce no reaction at all**, so no colour change on blotter means it is not LSD. That single fact is the reason to own it. Two practical notes: Ehrlich is slow and can take up to 20 minutes to develop, and it confirms the indole class without separating members of it, so it cannot tell LSD from DMT, nor N,N-DMT from 5-MeO-DMT. Colours in the [Ehrlich colour chart](/blog/ehrlich-reagent-color-chart.html); it ships as the [LSD kit](/lsd-kit).

**Morris** is the current ketamine reagent and is also two-part, Morris A and Morris B used together on one sample. It ships as the [ketamine kit](/ketamine-kit). More detail in [how to test ketamine](/blog/how-to-test-ketamine.html).

## The confirmers

**Froehde** backs up a Marquis result and helps separate look-alike reactions. It is in the MDMA kit for exactly that reason: screen with Marquis, discriminate with Simon's, confirm with Froehde. See the [Froehde colour chart](/blog/froehde-reagent-color-chart.html) or the [Froehde kit](/froehde-kit).

**Liebermann** is useful on cocaine and on 2C-B, and it ships inside the cocaine kit. It is the reagent that does the real work for 2C-B, where Marquis is comparatively uninformative. Colours in the [Liebermann colour chart](/blog/liebermann-reagent-color-chart.html), or the [Liebermann kit](/liebermann-kit) alone. See also [how to test 2C-B](/blog/how-to-test-2cb.html).

**Mandelin** was historically used for ketamine, but DanceSafe now describes that reaction as inconsistent and prone to overlapping with other drugs, which is why Morris replaced it for that job. A lot of older guidance online has not caught up. Mandelin still has uses, and its colours are in the [Mandelin colour chart](/blog/mandelin-reagent-color-chart.html). One quirk worth knowing: Mandelin turning cloudy yellow within a few weeks is normal ageing, not a fault.

**Folin** rounds out the complete set. It is the least commonly reached for of the nine, and it is the clearest example of a reagent most individual buyers will never open.

## Why running two is not wasteful

A common misreading is that using several reagents on one sample is belt-and-braces duplication. It is not. Each bottle is answering a different question, and the answers compound:

- **Marquis** narrows the class
- **Simon's** separates MDMA from MDA inside that class
- **Froehde** confirms and catches look-alike reactions

That sequence is three separate questions, not one question asked three times. The practical consequence for buying: a full Marquis plus Simon's plus Froehde workup on one sample consumes one test's worth from **three** bottles at once, so a kit covers fewer complete workups than the per-bottle figure suggests. [How many tests you get from one kit](/blog/how-many-tests-per-reagent-kit.html) covers the arithmetic.

## So how many should you buy?

Be honest with yourself about what you actually test, because that is the whole answer.

**If you test one substance**, buy the kit matched to it and stop. One bottle performs 50 to 75 tests, which for most people is more than a year of use. The [MDMA kit](/mdma-kit), [cocaine kit](/cocaine-kit), [LSD kit](/lsd-kit) and [ketamine kit](/ketamine-kit) each bundle the reagents that substance actually needs. Which one fits which substance is laid out in [our test kit comparison](/which-test-kit.html).

**If you test two or three substances**, look at whether the individual kits overlap before buying a bundle. Marquis appears in more than one kit, so buying two kits can mean buying Marquis twice.

**If you test across several different classes**, or you are supplying a group or a harm reduction org, the [standard set of six](/standard-kit) or the [complete set of nine](/kits) starts making sense. This is the genuine use case for a bundle, and it is a narrower one than the product page implies.

**Whatever you buy, add [fentanyl test strips](/fentanyl-strips) separately.** No reagent detects fentanyl at the doses that kill people, and strips are single-use, one strip per sample, so the one that ships in a kit covers exactly one test. Remember the direction: **one line is positive, two lines is negative.**

## The takeaway

Nine reagents exist because nine different questions exist, not because you have nine questions. Buy for the substance in front of you, learn what a blank result means, and put the difference toward strips, which are the consumable you will actually run out of.
