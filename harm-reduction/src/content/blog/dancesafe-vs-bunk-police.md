DanceSafe and Bunk Police are the two names most people hit when shopping for a reagent test kit. Both sell standard reagent formulations, and **no published, independent head-to-head test shows that one company's reagents are more accurate than the other's.** Anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing. What separates them is reagent range, what comes in the box, where they ship, and where the money goes.

**Disclosure first: Rave Wellness earns an affiliate commission when you buy through our DanceSafe links, and earns nothing from Bunk Police.** We have a financial reason to prefer one of these companies, and you should weigh what follows with that in mind. We wrote it so a Bunk Police customer would read it and find it accurate. Where Bunk Police is the better choice, we say so.

## Quick answers

**Is one company's reagent more accurate?** No published independent comparison exists. Both sell formulations in use for decades, and neither publishes third-party validation of its own product. Any claim that one brand "reads truer" is opinion, not finding.

**What is the biggest real difference?** Structure and range. DanceSafe is a nonprofit whose sales fund harm reduction work. Bunk Police is a for-profit with a wider catalogue: separation kits, purity kits, a mail-in lab service and the Hofmann reagent, none of them offered by DanceSafe.

**Which ships better internationally?** Neither, in practice. Both ship from the US and both publish the same list of 17 countries they cannot reach.

**Does either include fentanyl strips with a reagent kit?** Not with their MDMA kits. DanceSafe includes strips with its cocaine, LSD and ketamine kits but not the MDMA kit. Bunk Police sells strips separately across the board.

## What each one is

**DanceSafe** is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit registered under EIN 94-3365608, founded in 1998. Its own about page states that it produces reagents in-house in northern California, that it is the only nonprofit drug checking kit manufacturer in America, and that it provides free on-site drug checking at events with producer consent.

**[Bunk Police](https://bunkpolice.com/)** is a private operation founded by a person working under the pseudonym Adam Auctor, who started by testing for free at North American festivals. It does not describe itself as a nonprofit and is generally understood to be for-profit. Its stated mission is reducing overdose deaths through transparency about the supply.

If you want to buy from the nonprofit that also runs free drug checking, that is DanceSafe, and that is a legitimate reason to pick it. What nonprofit status does **not** tell you is anything about chemistry. A 501(c)(3) letter is a tax classification, not a quality certification, and we are not going to argue otherwise.

## Reagent range: nine each, but not the same nine

Both list nine reagents. Eight overlap.

| Reagent | DanceSafe | Bunk Police |
|---|---|---|
| Marquis, Mecke, Mandelin, Simon's | Yes | Yes |
| Froehde, Liebermann, Ehrlich, Morris | Yes | Yes |
| **Folin** | **Yes** | No |
| **Hofmann** | No | **Yes** |

That is the entire difference in range, and it cuts both ways. Want **Hofmann**, a secondary indole reagent some people run alongside Ehrlich on blotter? Only Bunk Police stocks it. Want **Folin**, used to flag piperazines? Only DanceSafe stocks it. Neither gap is a knock on either company, but it settles the purchase for anyone needing a specific reagent.

Both now use **Morris** as the ketamine reagent rather than Mandelin, matching current practice. Our [reagent color chart hub](/reagent-chart.html) covers how to read each of these, whoever made the bottle.

## What is in a comparable kit

Take the most common purchase, an MDMA kit.

- **DanceSafe MDMA kit**: Marquis, Simon's A, Simon's B and Froehde. No Mecke. It ships with one fentanyl test strip and a 10 mg micro scoop.
- **Bunk Police MDMA Spot Kit Package**: Marquis, Simon's and Mecke, per their own [how to test MDMA](https://bunkpolice.com/how-to-test-mdma/) page, which states plainly that fentanyl strips are sold separately.

Both do the essential job the same way: Marquis to screen, Simon's to separate MDMA from MDA. The third reagent differs, Froehde versus Mecke, and neither choice is wrong. Bunk Police also suggests Mandelin for PMA and PMMA. Our [MDMA testing walkthrough](/blog/how-to-test-mdma.html) explains what each reagent does.

Both sell fentanyl, xylazine and nitazene strips, so coverage is level. DanceSafe bundles a strip into its cocaine, LSD and ketamine kits and sells strips in bulk boxes with separate printed instruction sheets, aimed at chapters and community distributors. Bunk Police keeps strips separate across the board. Either way, read our [fentanyl strip guide](/blog/how-to-use-fentanyl-test-strips.html) first: the common failures are dilution and read-time errors, not brand errors.

## Shipping

We checked both shipping pages directly. Both ship from the US, and both publish the **same list of 17 countries they cannot reach**: Brazil, China, Colombia, Germany, Hong Kong, Israel, Malaysia, Mexico, Portugal, Russia, Singapore, South Africa, Spain, Taiwan, Thailand, United Arab Emirates and Vietnam. Both put customs duties on the customer, both warn that undelivered international USPS orders are not refundable, both ship in plain brown boxes with a discreet return address, and neither accepts returns.

Bunk Police's homepage claims "successful shipment to over 100 countries." DanceSafe makes no comparable claim. That is marketing, not a verified figure, and given identical exclusion lists we would not read it as broader reach. Outside the US, check your own customs rules and look for a regional supplier first.

## What Bunk Police has that DanceSafe does not

**On reference material Bunk Police is genuinely stronger, and this is its clearest advantage.** Its site hosts a free color guide booklet for each of its nine reagents, a public reaction video library presented as companion material to its book *Chromatic Reactions: The Art of Testing Drugs*, and a harm reduction app, and printed booklets ship with the kits. DanceSafe supplies instructions and color charts but has no equivalent video library.

It also runs product lines DanceSafe does not compete in at all:

- **Skylab separation kits**: silica cards, developing liquid and a black light, a home version of thin-layer chromatography meant to separate a mixed sample before you test the parts. Reagents cannot do this, because a strong reaction masks a weaker one.
- **QTest purity kits**: sold to estimate how much of a substance is present once identity is confirmed. Spot tests give identity, not quantity.
- **Mail-in lab testing**: their Transparency Testing service accepts powders, pills, crystals, blotters and liquids, listing FTIR, LC-MS, NMR and NIR as methods.

We have no independent verification of how well Skylab or QTest perform, and neither does anyone else publicly. Treat them as products addressing real gaps, not validated instruments.

## Is there published evidence either is better?

No. Neither company publishes third-party validation of its own reagents, and we found no independent head-to-head comparison of the two. Both sell long-established formulations, and reagent chemistry is not proprietary.

The failure modes are identical whoever sold you the bottle. Reagents do not give dose or purity, a dominant substance can mask a minor one, and nothing in a reagent bottle detects fentanyl. Heat and age degrade them, and Bunk Police's own FAQ advises against using a kit over a year old, sound guidance for either brand. See our post on whether [reagent kits expire](/blog/do-reagent-test-kits-expire.html) and the limits on our [MDMA page](/mdma.html).

## Which should you buy

Pick by situation, not by verdict.

- **US buyer who wants to buy from a nonprofit**: DanceSafe. The nonprofit structure and free event drug checking are real, and this is the one axis where the difference is unambiguous. Start with its [kits](/kits).
- **You want the widest toolset, video references or a lab option**: Bunk Police. Separation kits, purity kits, the video library and mail-in lab have no counterpart at DanceSafe.
- **You need one specific reagent**: Hofmann means Bunk Police, Folin means DanceSafe. Nothing else to weigh.
- **Outside the US**: close to a coin flip, and both may be beaten by a regional supplier.
- **Buying strips in volume for a program**: DanceSafe's bulk boxes and printed instruction sheets are built for it.
- **New, and you just want to test your pills**: either works. Buy the kit matching the substance you use, add fentanyl strips, and read the instructions before the night rather than during it. Our [which test kit guide](/which-test-kit.html) and [test kits page](/test-kits.html) walk through it.

The dishonest version of this post concludes that the company paying us wins everywhere. It does not. DanceSafe wins on where the money goes; Bunk Police wins on breadth of product and reference material. On the question most people actually have, whether the chemicals are better, there is no evidence either way, and we would rather say that than invent a difference.
