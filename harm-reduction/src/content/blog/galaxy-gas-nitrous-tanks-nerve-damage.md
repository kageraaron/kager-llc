**Galaxy Gas is not chemically different from the nitrous in a whipped cream charger. What changed is the container, and the container is the danger.** A classic whippet charger holds 8 grams. The flavored tanks that went viral in 2024 hold 580 grams and up, roughly 72 chargers in a single purchase. Nerve damage from nitrous is driven almost entirely by how much you inhale and how often, so a product that makes 300 gram sessions effortless moved a lot of people from occasional user to case report in months rather than years.

## What actually changed

Recreational nitrous lived in 8 gram steel chargers for decades, and buying a hundred of them is conspicuous, expensive per gram, and tedious to crack one at a time. Around 2023 and 2024, brands including Galaxy Gas, Baking Bad, Cosmic Gas and MassGass began selling large flavored canisters through vape shops, gas stations and Amazon. Cost per gram collapsed and per-session dose rose by an order of magnitude, because a tank has no natural stopping point the way a box of chargers does. Galaxy Gas halted its own direct online sales in September 2024 once the trend drew national attention; competing brands are still on shelves.

The flavoring has no published inhalation toxicology behind it, so nobody knows what the additives do to a lung. The documented effect is behavioral: flavoring makes large volumes more palatable, and volume is what hurts you.

## The dose response is the whole story

Nitrous irreversibly oxidizes the cobalt atom at the center of vitamin B12, shutting down methionine synthase and stalling myelin maintenance. Every gram you inhale permanently disables some of your active B12; your liver stores are the buffer, and tanks empty that buffer fast. Our post on [nitrous oxide and B12 nerve damage](/blog/nitrous-oxide-b12-nerve-damage.html) has the full mechanism.

The clinical picture tracks the dose. Largeau and colleagues, in the *European Journal of Neurology* in 2022, studied 20 hospitalized patients with a median age of 19. Median use was 100 cartridges per occasion, range 5 to 960, and higher cumulative dose correlated with more extensive limb damage (p = 0.042). Their conclusion was that no safe recreational exposure level exists. Median time from first use to neurological symptoms in that French cohort was six months, with some patients presenting in under a month.

Tshagharyan's 2025 series in *Neurological Research and Practice* covered 20 patients with a median age of 21 and a mean daily use of 2,500 grams, roughly 312 balloons a day. All had sensory deficits, 85 percent had gait disturbance, 73 percent had spinal cord lesions on MRI. A 2,500 gram daily habit is implausible with chargers and trivially easy with a tank.

Evidence tier: case series, and no randomized trial will ever be run on this. The biochemistry is unambiguous and the dose relationship holds across cohorts.

## A normal B12 result does not mean you are fine

This is the part that gets people sent home. Serum B12 measures total circulating cobalamin, including the molecules nitrous has already oxidized into uselessness, and inactivated B12 still shows up on the test. Two functional markers tell the real story: **methylmalonic acid (MMA)**, which rises when B12-dependent metabolism stalls, and **homocysteine**, which accumulates when methionine synthase is offline.

In Tshagharyan's cohort, serum B12 was normal in 95 percent of patients while homocysteine was elevated in 100 percent and MMA in 95 percent. Meißner's 2025 study of 23 patients across five German hospitals found B12 abnormal in only 35 percent, MMA in 95 percent, homocysteine in 89 percent. Hassing's 70-patient series found 91 percent of patients with normal B12 had elevated MMA.

If your B12 comes back at 400, that is not reassurance. Ask for MMA and homocysteine by name.

## Symptoms that mean stop now

These build over days to weeks, and they are the only warning you get before the damage becomes harder to reverse.

- Tingling, numbness or pins and needles in fingers and toes, usually symmetrical
- Feeling unsteady walking, especially in the dark or with your eyes closed
- An electric shock down the spine when you bend your neck forward (Lhermitte sign)
- Clumsiness with buttons, keys, a phone screen
- Later: leg weakness, stiffness, bladder problems

Sensory symptoms come first in nearly every published series. Tingling hands or feet after tank use means stop and get evaluated, not wait and see.

## The tank itself, before the B12 ever comes into it

**Frostbite.** Nitrous leaves a pressurized tank extremely cold. A 2025 review in the *European Burn Journal* found most published nitrous frostbite cases clustered in 2022 to 2024, commonly on the inner thighs from resting the tank there, some needing excision and skin grafting. Inhaling from the valve can freeze lips, mouth and airway.

**Barotrauma.** Inhaling straight from a pressurized tank has caused pneumothorax and pneumomediastinum, gas forced through torn lung tissue into the chest cavity. A balloon prevents both this and the frostbite by dropping the gas to ambient pressure first, which is why it is the one rule worth keeping if you keep only one.

**Hypoxia.** Nitrous displaces oxygen and blunts the panic reflex that would otherwise make you pull away. Sustained inhalation from a mask, a bag over the head, or a sealed car is what kills people. Yockey and Hoopsick reported in *JAMA Network Open* in 2025 that US nitrous poisoning deaths rose from 23 in 2010 to 156 in 2023, 1,240 in total among people aged 15 to 74. Read that honestly: the rise ran to 2018 and then plateaued, so the tank era's effect on mortality is not yet in published data.

## Where regulation stands

The FDA advised consumers not to inhale nitrous oxide products on 14 March 2025, naming Galaxy Gas, Whip-It!, Baking Bad, Cosmic Gas, HOTWHIP, InfusionMax, MassGass and Miami Magic, and citing nerve damage, blood clots, loss of consciousness, paralysis, psychiatric effects and death. ExoticWhip, FastGas and NITROX were added on 4 June 2025, and the warning covers any flavor in any size canister, tank or charger.

Louisiana and Michigan restricted recreational sales in 2024; Alabama, California and Washington followed in 2025. Federally these products remain legal as a food propellant, which is why they are still on gas station shelves.

## If you are going to use anyway

- **Balloon, always. Never the valve.**
- **Set a hard ceiling before you start,** and count. A few balloons is a categorically different exposure from a session that empties a tank.
- **Sit or lie down.** Fainting is common and falls are a leading cause of nitrous ER visits.
- **Never in a car, a closed room, a tent, or with anything over your head.** Oxygen displacement is what causes the deaths.
- **Space sessions by weeks, not hours.** Frequency is what builds cumulative dose.
- **Ask for MMA and homocysteine,** not just serum B12, if you use more than occasionally.
- **Oral B12 is not armor.** Methylcobalamin maintains baseline stores and low baseline B12 is the biggest risk multiplier, so it is worth taking. It has never been shown to prevent nitrous myeloneuropathy in heavy users, and we are not going to imply otherwise.
- **Do not stack with alcohol, ketamine, GHB or opioids.** Sedation plus hypoxia is how people stop breathing. Check combinations with the [drug interaction checker](/interactions.html).

## If you catch it early

Treatment is abstinence plus intramuscular hydroxocobalamin, not oral cyanocobalamin. At three months in the Hassing series, 31 percent had fully recovered, 50 percent had mild residual symptoms and 19 percent still had disabling deficits; in Meißner's cohort, four of the seven patients with follow-up showed no meaningful improvement.

Roughly a third recover completely, and they are overwhelmingly the ones who stopped early. Tell your doctor exactly what you used and how much, because they will not order MMA and homocysteine if they think they are looking at an unexplained neuropathy.
