Most festival medical emergencies are preventable, and the prevention happens at home before you leave rather than at the venue. This rave packing list is organised around what actually goes wrong at these events: overheating, hyponatremia, hearing damage, and taking something that is not what you were told it was. Each item earns its place by preventing one of those. Several of the most important things to bring cost nothing at all.

## Quick answers

**What should I pack for a rave?** Test kit and fentanyl strips (used at home before you go), high-fidelity earplugs, electrolytes, a water container you can refill, and a charged phone with your buddy's number. Naloxone if anyone in your group uses opioids or takes pressed pills.

**What is the single most important item?** Fentanyl test strips, because they address the failure mode that kills people fastest and they are the cheapest item on the list.

**Do I need to buy everything on this list?** No. The buddy plan, knowing where the medical tent is, the Good Samaritan conversation, and naloxone in most US states all cost nothing.

**Can I test at the festival instead?** You can if on-site checking is available, but coverage is inconsistent and lines are long. Testing at home is faster, more private, and leaves you time to act on a bad result.

## The rave packing list (quick version)

Your pre-event checklist in one scan, with details in the sections below:

- **Test everything**: [fentanyl test strips](/fentanyl-strips) plus a reagent kit, done at home before you go
- **Plan [hydration](/hydration-pack)**: about 500 ml per hour with electrolytes if dancing, 250 ml per hour resting
- **Prepare for heat**: light clothing, locate the chill-out area, schedule cooling breaks
- **Pack [earplugs](/earplugs)**: high-fidelity, and bring two pairs

## What actually goes wrong at a rave

Packing lists usually read as a pile of products. It is more useful to start from the failure modes, because that tells you which items are non-negotiable and which are comfort:

- **The substance is not what you were told.** Handled by testing at home.
- **Hyperthermia.** MDMA impairs thermoregulation, so you stop noticing how hot you are. Handled by breaks, cooling, and the chill-out area.
- **Hyponatremia.** Drinking large volumes of plain water while on MDMA dilutes blood sodium and has been fatal. Handled by electrolytes, not by drinking more.
- **Noise exposure.** A single loud night produces measurable temporary threshold shift. Handled by earplugs.
- **Nobody knows what you took.** The failure mode that turns a manageable situation into an emergency. Handled by a conversation that costs nothing.

Everything below maps to one of those.

## Test your substances before you go

On-site drug checking exists at some festivals, but coverage is inconsistent and lines can be long. Testing at home is faster, more private, and gives you time to make a decision while you still have options.

**Minimum testing setup:**

- [Fentanyl test strips](/fentanyl-strips), relevant for any powder or pill regardless of claimed substance. Fentanyl has been detected in MDMA, cocaine, ketamine, and pressed pills sold as various substances. These are single-use, one strip per sample, so buy more than you think you need.
- A reagent kit matched to what you are testing. The [MDMA kit](/mdma-kit) contains Marquis, the two-part Simon's, and Froehde. Simon's is the one that matters most, because it separates MDMA from MDA. The [cocaine](/cocaine-kit), [LSD](/lsd-kit) and [ketamine](/ketamine-kit) kits each ship with a fentanyl strip too. Those starter strips are single-use, so add more if you are testing more than one sample.
- A [spot plate](/spot-plate) so you are reacting samples on ceramic rather than on something that will stain or react itself.
- A [micro scoop](/micro-scoop) to take a consistent small sample instead of guessing.
- A [milligram-accurate scale](/mg-scale) for powders, with calibration weights. Weigh doses at home, not in a dark venue.
- [Xylazine test strips](/xylazine-strips) if you are testing anything from the opioid or pressed-pill supply, since naloxone does not reverse xylazine sedation.

If you are testing several different substances, the [complete set](/kits) covers more ground than buying kits individually. A positive fentanyl result is a reason not to use, not a reason to use less.

## Hydration: more complex than "drink water"

Both dehydration and overhydration have caused deaths at rave events. The correct approach:

- **Dancing actively:** ~500 ml per hour, with electrolytes, not plain water
- **Resting:** ~250 ml per hour maximum
- **MDMA specifically** causes the body to retain water. Drinking excessive plain water while on MDMA causes hyponatremia (dangerously low sodium), which has been fatal. Always use electrolytes.

Electrolyte powder of any brand works, as does a pinch of salt with coconut water. DanceSafe sells [hydration packs](/hydration-pack) if you want one that ships with the rest of your order. Avoid alcohol as a primary hydration source, which is obvious but worth stating.

More detail in our [festival heat and hydration guide](/blog/festival-heat-hydration.html).

## Temperature management

Hyperthermia is a leading cause of MDMA-related deaths. MDMA impairs thermoregulation, meaning you stop feeling how hot you are. Practical prevention:

- Take 10 to 15 minute breaks from dancing every hour
- Find the chill-out area before you need it, identifying it at the start of the event
- Wet cloths on the back of the neck, wrists, and temples cool core temperature quickly
- Know the signs: stopping sweating (paradoxical, when overheating reaches a critical threshold), confusion, collapse

If someone collapses from suspected heat stroke: **move them to a cool space, remove excess clothing, apply cool water to skin, call emergency services.** Do not leave them alone.

## Hearing protection

A single night at a festival without earplugs produces measurable temporary threshold shifts. Repeated exposure causes permanent hearing loss that accumulates gradually and is irreversible. A randomised trial at an outdoor festival found earplugs reduced temporary threshold shift roughly fivefold compared with unprotected listening ([PMID 27054284](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27054284/)), which makes this the best-evidenced item on the entire list.

High-fidelity earplugs, such as the [DanceSafe EarPeace plugs](/earplugs), reduce volume across frequencies evenly rather than distorting sound quality the way foam does. Loop and Etymotic make comparable high-fidelity plugs if you already own a pair.

Bring two pairs, because you will lose one. See our [hearing health guide](/hearing.html) and [do earplugs ruin the music](/blog/do-earplugs-ruin-music-at-raves.html).

## If you are snorting anything

Shared straws and rolled banknotes transmit hepatitis C, which survives on surfaces longer than most people assume, and rolled notes are abrasive enough to cause the small nasal bleeds that make transmission possible. Bring your own tube and do not share it. DanceSafe sells [single-use crisp tubes](/crisp-tube) designed for this. Details in our guide on [sharing straws and hepatitis C](/blog/sharing-straws-hepatitis-c.html).

## What to tell a friend (and have them tell you)

Before dosing, tell a trusted sober-ish person:

1. What you took and approximately how much
2. When you took it
3. What interactions you have checked for
4. Where you will meet if separated
5. That Good Samaritan laws apply, so calling for help will not get them arrested

The Fireside Project (62-FIRESIDE) provides free peer support by phone during or after difficult psychedelic experiences. Save the number before you go.

## Emergency preparedness

Know where the medical tent is. Know the festival's drug amnesty policy, since most large festivals have one. Know that MDMA and stimulant overdoses look very different from opioid overdoses: naloxone does not reverse MDMA toxicity, but it is essential for fentanyl exposure.

If you carry naloxone, know how to use it. NEXT Distro ships free naloxone to most US states, and many local harm reduction programmes hand it out at no cost, so this is one item you should not be paying for.

## What this actually costs

Worth being clear about, because packing lists tend to imply everything requires a purchase:

**Costs nothing:** the buddy conversation, locating the medical tent and chill-out area on arrival, knowing your state's Good Samaritan law, saving the Fireside number, scheduling cooling breaks, and naloxone in most US states.

**Costs something, once:** earplugs and a scale last for years. A reagent bottle performs 50 to 75 tests, so the per-test cost is far lower than most people assume when they see the bottle price.

**Costs something, repeatedly:** fentanyl and xylazine test strips are single-use. This is the one line item to over-buy rather than ration.

The most effective harm reduction happens before the event. Testing, planning hydration, identifying your support person, and knowing where the medical tent is takes about thirty minutes, and most of it is free. The risk reduction is substantial.
