If you took MDMA (molly, ecstasy) and never came up, the cause is almost always one of five things: **a medication that blocks it, using MDMA too recently, a substance that isn't what you were told, a dose too low, or absorption that's just slow.** Only one of those is fixed by taking more, and taking more when you can't feel anything is the most dangerous mistake in MDMA use.

## Quick answers

**Why didn't I feel my molly?** Most often: an SSRI or SNRI blocking MDMA's mechanism, using MDMA again within a few weeks, a sample containing little or no MDMA, or a dose too low for your body weight.

**How long should I wait before deciding it isn't working?** At least 2 hours. Onset runs 30 to 60 minutes on an empty stomach and up to 90 minutes after a meal, with peak plasma levels around 1.5 to 2.5 hours.

**Does ketamine stop you coming up?** It doesn't block MDMA pharmacologically, but dissociation and sedation flatten the emotional come-up, so the roll registers as muted rather than absent.

**Can I just redose until I feel it?** No. This is the main pathway to MDMA overdose and hyperthermia. Blood levels climb faster than the dose you added, while subjective effects climb slower.

**Does alcohol kill a roll?** Controlled data doesn't support that. Alcohol raised MDMA plasma levels 13% in a human trial and made euphoria last longer. What it does is blur your read on your own state.

## SSRIs and SNRIs: the most common reason by far

MDMA works by reversing the serotonin transporter (SERT), dumping serotonin into the synapse. SSRIs and SNRIs occupy that exact transporter, so MDMA loses its main route in.

In a double-blind crossover trial, citalopram pretreatment markedly reduced most of MDMA's psychological effects in 16 volunteers ([PMID 10731626](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10731626/)). Five days of fluoxetine attenuated most positive subjective effects in recreational users ([PMID 17047932](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17047932/)). A 2022 systematic review of 40 publications, 26 of them RCTs, found consistent attenuation across citalopram, fluoxetine, and paroxetine ([PMID 35253070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35253070/)).

If you're on an SSRI or SNRI and felt nothing, that's your answer, and **the fix is not more MDMA**. The blunting persists for weeks after stopping, because receptor changes outlast the drug. Full detail in our [MDMA and antidepressants guide](/blog/mdma-ssri-interaction.html). Do not stop psychiatric medication to roll.

## You used MDMA too recently

MDMA releases serotonin from existing stores, and those stores take weeks to refill. Use MDMA two weeks after your last session and there is physically less to release, so the same dose delivers a thinner, more stimulant-flavored experience.

Acute tolerance shows up within a single night. When volunteers took a second MDMA dose 2 hours after the first, plasma concentrations ran slightly higher than predicted, yet euphoria and stimulation came in **lower than expected** ([PMID 23142957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23142957/)). More drug in the blood, less feeling. That is why the 4 to 6 week spacing rule exists. See [how long to wait between MDMA sessions](/blog/mdma-how-long-between-uses.html).

## It probably isn't MDMA

An analysis of 4,719 samples submitted to a US drug-checking service between 1999 and 2023 found **only about 48% contained MDMA alone**, more than half were misrepresented in some way, and 199 unique adulterants turned up ([PMID 39437494](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39437494/)). Purity has improved since 2017, but "sold as MDMA" is still not "is MDMA." A sample can also be real MDMA at a useless dose: pressed pills range from under 50 mg to over 250 mg with no external clue.

Reagent testing takes two minutes. Marquis turns purple to black for MDMA and stays yellow or orange for common substitutes; Simon's distinguishes MDMA from MDA. A [DanceSafe MDMA testing kit](/mdma-kit) covers Marquis, Mecke, Simon's, and fentanyl strips. Our walkthrough: [how to test MDMA](/blog/how-to-test-mdma.html).

## Dose, body weight, and a full stomach

Common recreational doses land around 1 to 1.5 mg per kg of body weight. A 100 mg pill that is solid for a 55 kg person is light for a 95 kg person, and if you took a fraction of a pill to be cautious, a flat night is the expected outcome. See our [MDMA dosing guide](/blog/mdma-dosing-guide.html).

Food doesn't destroy a roll, it delays it: onset shifts from roughly 20 to 30 minutes on an empty stomach to 75 to 90 minutes after a large meal. **The come-up that "never happened" at T+50 has arrived at T+90 many times**, usually after someone already redosed. Full timings in our [MDMA timeline](/blog/how-long-does-mdma-last.html).

## Ketamine and other dissociatives

No controlled study has given ketamine with MDMA, so this is mechanistic reasoning, not trial evidence. Ketamine is an NMDA antagonist producing dissociation and detachment from bodily and emotional signals even at subanesthetic doses ([PMID 8122957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8122957/)). MDMA's come-up is largely an emotional and bodily event: warmth, openness, body load. Dissociation dampens exactly that channel.

People bump ketamine through the come-up, read the roll as weak, then redose MDMA in a state where they can't assess themselves. If you're combining, take the MDMA first and leave ketamine until the plateau. More in our [ketamine guide](/ketamine.html).

## Other medications, including the ones that do the opposite

- **Antipsychotics**: Ketanserin, a 5-HT2A/2C antagonist, reduced MDMA-induced perceptual changes and emotional excitation ([PMID 10989266](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10989266/)). Haloperidol, a D2 antagonist, blunted MDMA's positive mood ([PMID 10871712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10871712/)). Quetiapine blocks both 5-HT2A and D2 and is strongly sedating, so a flat roll is expected, though it has never been tested with MDMA directly.
- **Bupropion**: Often assumed to block MDMA. It does the reverse, raising MDMA plasma concentrations and prolonging subjective effects while reducing the heart rate response ([PMID 25655950](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25655950/)).
- **Stimulants and antihistamines**: No controlled human MDMA data for either. Amphetamine on board can make MDMA read as "just speedy," and sedating antihistamines can flatten it.
- **CYP2D6 variation**: Less relevant than commonly claimed. CYP2D6 handles no more than 30% of MDMA metabolism ([PMID 15910012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15910012/)), and among 139 pooled subjects, poor metabolizers had **higher** peak levels and faster onset, not weaker effects ([PMID 27253829](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27253829/)). Genotype explains toxicity risk better than a dud.

## Alcohol, sleep, illness, and mindset

Alcohol gets blamed constantly and the controlled evidence doesn't back it. In a randomized crossover trial, MDMA plus alcohol produced longer-lasting euphoria than either alone, and MDMA plasma levels rose 13% ([PMID 11752122](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11752122/)). The real problem is that alcohol hides both what you're feeling and how impaired you are, the exact condition under which people redose badly. See [MDMA and alcohol](/blog/mdma-and-alcohol.html).

Sleep deprivation, illness, and low expectations have **no direct human MDMA trial data**. Exhaustion and illness shift your baseline mood and body sensation, and MDMA is experienced relative to that baseline. Anxious self-monitoring ("is it working yet?") delays when people notice a come-up. Plausible contributors, not established mechanisms.

## The dangerous response: redosing until you feel something

**MDMA has non-linear pharmacokinetics.** Small increases in dose produce disproportionately large rises in plasma concentration, because the metabolic pathway saturates ([PMID 10671903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10671903/)). Doubling what you have taken can more than double blood levels.

Stack that against acute tolerance: after a second dose, subjective effects come in **below** what blood levels predict ([PMID 23142957](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23142957/)). The curves separate. Your feelings plateau while exposure keeps climbing, so someone chasing a come-up across three or four doses can reach genuinely toxic levels while still reporting they barely feel it.

The endpoint is hyperthermia. MDMA raises body temperature, and in a hot, crowded room with sustained dancing, heat production overwhelms heat loss. Hyperpyrexia leading to rhabdomyolysis and multi-organ failure is the classic presentation in MDMA fatalities ([PMID 16595612](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16595612/)). It is dose-related, and it does not require you to feel high.

**If you don't feel anything:**

1. Wait a full 2 hours before concluding anything.
2. If you redose at all, do it once, before the 2 hour mark, at half the original amount or less.
3. If you still feel nothing after that, accept the night as a loss. Do not take a third dose.
4. Watch for hot dry skin, confusion, rigidity, or a temperature above 38°C in anyone. That's an emergency: cool them down and call emergency services.

## Bottom line

A failed roll usually has a boring explanation: an antidepressant, a recent session, a weak or fake sample, or a dose still absorbing. **None of those are fixed by taking more, and trying to fix them that way is the main route to MDMA overdose and hyperthermia.**
