A cocaine heart attack does not require blocked arteries, a family history, or years of use. **In the first 60 minutes after use, the risk of myocardial infarction rises about 24-fold above baseline**, per a case-crossover analysis of 3,946 heart attack patients in *Circulation* ([PMID 10351966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10351966/)). That spike hits people in their twenties and thirties with clean arteries, because cocaine damages the heart through mechanisms unrelated to plaque. National survey data suggest **one in four non-fatal heart attacks in Americans aged 18 to 45 is attributable to frequent cocaine use** ([PMID 11157713](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11157713/)). Chest pain after cocaine means an emergency room, and staff need to know what you took.

## Quick answers

**Can cocaine cause a heart attack in a young, healthy person?** Yes. Cocaine constricts the coronary arteries while raising the heart's oxygen demand, starving the heart muscle even when the arteries are structurally normal. It has happened on a first use.

**How long after using cocaine is the risk highest?** Risk peaks in the first hour ([PMID 10351966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10351966/)). In a 246-patient emergency department study, the median time from cocaine use to chest pain onset was about 60 minutes ([PMID 7614278](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7614278/)).

**Is cocaine chest pain always a heart attack?** No. In that study, 5.7% of cocaine-associated chest pain patients had a confirmed heart attack. But **no bedside sign reliably separates that 5.7% from everyone else**, which is why all of it gets worked up.

**What should I do if I get chest pain after using cocaine?** Go to an emergency room and tell them you used cocaine, how much, and when. Do not drive yourself, and do not take more cocaine or alcohol to ride it out.

## Why cocaine damages hearts with no plaque in them

**Oxygen demand goes up.** Cocaine blocks norepinephrine reuptake, leaving the heart bathed in its own stress hormones. Heart rate, blood pressure, and contraction force all rise, so the muscle needs more oxygen than it did minutes ago.

**Oxygen supply drops at the same time.** In a *New England Journal of Medicine* catheterization study, intranasal cocaine at 2 mg/kg (comparable to recreational use) narrowed the left coronary artery by 8% to 12% and cut coronary blood flow, in patients who felt nothing at the time ([PMID 2573838](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2573838/)). The alpha-blocker phentolamine reversed it, confirming the effect runs through alpha-adrenergic receptors. A follow-up found constriction more than twice as severe at already-narrowed segments: 29% versus 13% ([PMID 2358608](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2358608/)).

**Blood clots more readily.** In a randomized, double-blind crossover trial in 14 healthy volunteers, intranasal cocaine roughly doubled platelet factor 4 and raised platelet microaggregates within 80 minutes, at doses the authors called typical of occasional users ([PMID 10814631](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10814631/)). A spasming artery plus activated platelets is how a clot forms in a vessel that was healthy an hour earlier.

**Chronic use rebuilds the heart badly.** Echocardiography in 30 chronic users versus 30 matched non-hypertensive controls found left ventricular mass index of 103 versus 77 g/m2 ([PMID 1832090](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1832090/)). In young, healthy regular users, left ventricular mass ran 18% higher alongside raised blood pressure and stiffer aortas, scaling with duration and frequency ([PMID 24717541](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24717541/)). Cardiac MRI in 94 asymptomatic users found abnormalities in 71%, with 30% showing heart muscle scarring ([PMID 24758161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24758161/)), and plaque builds faster too ([PMID 15795348](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15795348/)). A thickened, scarred ventricle is what turns a survivable arrhythmia into a fatal one, and how cocaine-induced cardiomyopathy develops, usually silently.

## The electrical problem: why the comedown is not safe either

Cocaine is a local anesthetic, so it blocks cardiac sodium channels, slowing conduction and widening the QRS complex on an ECG. It also blocks hERG potassium channels, prolonging the QT interval and setting up torsades de pointes, a lethal rhythm ([PMID 18815938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18815938/)). Sodium bicarbonate treats cocaine-induced QRS widening; antiarrhythmics that also block sodium channels can make it worse.

**Arrhythmia risk does not switch off when the high fades.** Sodium channel blockade outlasts the euphoria, which is why people arrest hours after their last line.

## Aortic dissection: the other reason cocaine chest pain gets taken seriously

An abrupt catecholamine surge can tear the aorta. At one urban hospital, 14 of 38 acute aortic dissections over two decades (37%) were cocaine-related, a mean of 12 hours after use ([PMID 11927528](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11927528/)). In the International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection, cocaine accounted for 1.8% of 3,584 dissections, in patients much younger than the rest ([PMID 24835037](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24835037/)). Tearing pain radiating to the back is a call-an-ambulance situation.

## What makes it worse: alcohol, adulterants, and stacking stimulants

**Alcohol.** Cocaine plus alcohol produces cocaethylene, a third active compound more cardiotoxic than cocaine that clears more slowly. In a two-site emergency department cohort, cardiac arrest occurred in 6.1% of cocaethylene-positive patients versus 0.67% of cocaine-only patients ([PMID 36000306](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36000306/)). See [cocaine and alcohol](/blog/cocaine-and-alcohol.html).

**Adulterants.** Levamisole, the veterinary dewormer in up to 79% of street cocaine ([PMID 33298746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33298746/)), drives vasculitis as well as white blood cell destruction ([levamisole guide](/blog/levamisole-in-cocaine.html)). Fentanyl showed up in 14.8% of powder cocaine samples in community drug checking ([PMID 37826988](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37826988/)); a [DanceSafe fentanyl test strip pack](/fentanyl-strips) with fentanyl strips is the cheapest risk reduction available, and our [guide to testing cocaine](/blog/how-to-test-cocaine.html) covers the method.

**Other stimulants.** MDMA and amphetamines stack the same cardiac load. See [mixing cocaine and MDMA](/blog/cocaine-and-mdma.html) and the [drug interaction checker](/interactions.html).

## Chest pain after cocaine: exactly what to do

1. **Stop using. Do not redose, do not drink.** More stimulant on a struggling heart turns a warning into an event.
2. **Get to an emergency room.** Call 911 or have someone drive you. Do not drive yourself.
3. **Tell them you used cocaine, how much, and when.** Say it unprompted, at triage.
4. **Say what else was involved:** alcohol, MDMA, opioids, prescriptions.

### Why disclosure changes your care

Once the team knows you used cocaine an hour ago, chest pain with a normal-looking ECG takes a different pathway: it changes what they test for (aortic dissection enters the picture), what they watch (QRS width, QT interval), and what they give you.

The American Heart Association scientific statement on cocaine-associated chest pain ([PMID 18347214](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18347214/)) built the standard approach around **benzodiazepines**, which lower heart rate, blood pressure, and anxiety at the source, plus **aspirin** for platelet activation and **nitroglycerin** for vasospasm. A small randomized trial found lorazepam plus nitroglycerin relieved chest pain significantly better than nitroglycerin alone, with no adverse events ([PMID 12563578](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12563578/)). Phentolamine is reserved for spasm that does not resolve.

**The beta-blocker question.** Classic teaching is to avoid beta-blockers acutely, because blocking beta receptors leaves alpha-mediated vasoconstriction unopposed and worsens coronary spasm. That comes from a catheterization study where intracoronary propranolol after cocaine further reduced coronary blood flow ([PMID 1971166](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1971166/)). Evidence since is mixed: retrospective cohorts of 348 and 331 patients found no excess harm, and in one, fewer heart attacks ([PMID 17583376](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17583376/), [PMID 20498415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20498415/)), and a 2017 review argued the unopposed alpha phenomenon is overstated ([PMID 28399647](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28399647/)). That debate is not yours to solve. **Your job is to give the team the information so they can make the call.** They cannot weigh a risk they do not know exists.

### Good Samaritan protections

Most US states have Good Samaritan laws protecting you from arrest or prosecution for drug possession when you seek emergency help for yourself or someone else. Coverage varies by state, and **hospitals are not law enforcement.** Clinicians ask about drug use because the answer changes the treatment, and what you tell them is protected health information. Withholding it only makes your care worse.

## Reducing the risk if you are going to use

None of this makes cocaine cardiac-safe. It reduces specific documented harms.

- **Skip the alcohol.** Cocaethylene is the most modifiable multiplier of cardiac risk here.
- **Use less per dose, wait longer between doses.** Vasospasm and platelet activation both track with peak plasma levels.
- **Do not stack stimulants,** including MDMA and amphetamines.
- **Test for fentanyl every time,** with a fresh strip per batch.
- **Know your baseline.** Hypertension, a known arrhythmia, or family history of sudden cardiac death under 50 raises your risk further.
- **Do not use alone,** and tell whoever is with you what you took.
- **Treat chest pain, arm or jaw pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, or a racing irregular heartbeat as an emergency.**

---

## The bottom line

Cocaine causes heart attacks in young people with normal arteries by squeezing the coronary vessels shut, demanding more work from the heart, and making blood clot more readily. That risk concentrates in the first hour and does not scale predictably with dose or experience. Chest pain after cocaine means an emergency room, and the most useful thing you can do there is say exactly what you took and when.
