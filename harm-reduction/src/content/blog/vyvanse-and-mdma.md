If you take 40 mg of Vyvanse at 10:45am and plan to take MDMA at midnight, you are not working with a clean window. That is 13.25 hours, and d-amphetamine (the active drug Vyvanse becomes) has a terminal half-life of about 10.39 hours, so somewhere in the region of **40 to 50 percent of your peak amphetamine level is still circulating** when the DJ comes on. MDMA is itself a substituted amphetamine with strong noradrenergic effects, so you would be stacking one stimulant on a substantial amount of another: additive heart rate and blood pressure, worse heat regulation in a hot room, a harder comedown, and possibly a blunted roll that tempts you into redosing. That does not mean the night is guaranteed to go badly. It means the margin is thinner than with a longer gap, and the two things that matter most are staying cool and not redosing.

## Quick answers

**How long does Vyvanse actually last?** Vyvanse itself is gone within an hour. It is cleaved into d-amphetamine, and that is what lasts: peak blood levels at about 3 hours, terminal half-life about 10.39 hours ([PMID 18991468](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18991468/)). A morning dose is meaningfully present well past midnight.

**Does the dose change the timing?** No. Elimination is first-order, so the half-life belongs to the drug, not the dose. A 40 mg and a 70 mg dose decay on the same clock.

**Is there a safe gap?** No validated number exists, because nobody has run the study. Getting past three or four half-lives (roughly 30 to 40 hours) leaves very little amphetamine, which in practice means skipping a dose on a planned day, not delaying it.

**What is the actual danger?** Overheating, mainly. Both drugs raise heat production and impair heat loss, and hyperthermia is the leading cause of acute MDMA deaths. Cardiovascular strain runs a close second.

**Should I just stop taking my prescription?** That is a real tradeoff, not a free move. If you need the medication to work, you need it. You can plan around it on a day you choose, and that decision is yours.

## The arithmetic, shown in the open

The pharmacokinetics come from an open-label single-dose study of lisdexamfetamine dimesylate in 6 healthy adult volunteers ([PMID 18991468](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18991468/), Krishnan 2008, *Clinical Drug Investigation*). Participants received a single 70 mg oral dose. The findings that matter here:

- **Intact lisdexamfetamine terminal half-life**: 0.47 hours. The prodrug is gone within an hour or two.
- **d-amphetamine tmax**: 3.00 hours. The active drug peaks about three hours after you swallow the capsule.
- **d-amphetamine terminal half-life**: 10.39 hours.
- **Elimination occurred as a first-order process.**

That last line is why this transfers to a 40 mg dose. First-order elimination means a constant *fraction* is cleared per unit time, not a constant amount, so the half-life does not shift with dose. The study used 70 mg, the question was about 40 mg, and the timing conclusion is identical.

**The simple version.** Dose at 10:45am, MDMA at 12:00am. That is 13.25 hours.

13.25 ÷ 10.39 = **1.28 half-lives**

0.5 ^ 1.28 = **0.41**, so about **41 percent** of peak remains.

**The more careful version.** Decay starts at peak, not at swallowing. Peak is 3 hours in, so 1:45pm. From 1:45pm to midnight is 10.25 hours.

10.25 ÷ 10.39 = **0.99 half-lives**

0.5 ^ 0.99 = **0.50**, so about **50 percent** of peak remains.

So the range is roughly 40 to 50 percent of peak, and the simpler calculation is, if anything, the optimistic one. To redo this yourself: take the hours between your dose and your planned MDMA, divide by 10.39, and raise 0.5 to that power. Four extra hours barely moves it. To get below 10 percent you need about 34 hours.

**Evidence tier: pharmacology-derived (tier 2).** This is a calculation from a published pharmacokinetic study, not a measured outcome. That study enrolled 6 healthy adults on a single dose and was not designed to answer questions about combining drugs. Urinary pH, body composition, and genetics all shift real-world clearance. Treat the number as an order-of-magnitude estimate: substantial, not trace.

## Why stacking MDMA on residual amphetamine is the risk

The specific combination of MDMA and prescribed amphetamine has not been tested in a controlled human trial. A review of MDMA's interactions with other psychoactive drugs ([PMID 21756931](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21756931/)) notes that although polydrug use is very common, the consequences of specific combinations have received relatively little attention. So the reasoning below comes from each drug's known effects plus the closest human evidence available.

That closest evidence is a double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study of methylphenidate and MDMA given alone or together in healthy volunteers ([PMID 24103254](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24103254/), Hysek 2014). Methylphenidate is not amphetamine, but the finding is directly relevant: the combination **did not produce more psychoactive effects than either drug alone, while its haemodynamic and adverse effects were significantly higher**. More strain, no more experience.

**Cardiovascular strain is additive.** Both drugs raise heart rate and blood pressure through noradrenaline, and those effects add up while you are dancing in a hot room, which is a cardiovascular load on its own. This is the main acute risk, and far more serious with an undiagnosed heart condition.

**Thermoregulation is the one that kills people.** MDMA increases metabolic heat production and constricts the blood vessels near your skin: more heat generated, less heat shed. Amphetamine does the same. Add a crowded room, sustained dancing, and no breaks, and core temperature climbs. Hyperthermia is the leading cause of acute MDMA deaths, so our guide to [staying cool and hydrated at festivals](/blog/festival-heat-hydration.html) matters more here than usual.

**Neurotoxicity risk tracks temperature.** A classic rat study found that small changes in ambient temperature caused large changes in both core body temperature and MDMA-induced serotonin neurotoxicity ([PMID 9634574](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9634574/)). Anything that raises core temperature plausibly raises that risk. See our breakdown of [MDMA neurotoxicity](/blog/mdma-neurotoxicity.html) for what the human evidence shows.

**The comedown stacks too.** MDMA depletes serotonin, and both drugs deplete dopamine and noradrenaline. The next day you get an MDMA comedown with an ADHD rebound layered on top, at the point where your medication is least likely to help. Our [MDMA comedown guide](/blog/mdma-comedown.html) covers recovery, but expect a rougher version.

**The roll may be weaker, which is its own risk.** People on regular stimulant therapy often report a flatter or delayed MDMA experience. That is what the mechanism predicts, since both drugs act on the same transporters and monoamine pools, and the methylphenidate study found no enhancement of MDMA's psychoactive effects. **No controlled trial has tested this in people on prescribed amphetamine**, so treat it as community practice plus plausible mechanism, not established fact. The danger is behavioural: a weak roll is the most common reason people redose.

## What a genuinely longer gap looks like

**Skip the dose on a planned day.** If the show is Saturday, not taking Saturday's dose puts you more than 30 hours out from Friday's, taking you from about 40 percent of peak down to close to nothing. This is the only option that meaningfully changes the maths.

**Or take it much earlier.** Moving a dose from 10:45am to 6:00am buys 4.75 hours, less than half a half-life, and takes the midnight figure from roughly 41 percent to roughly 29 percent. Better, not transformative.

**Skipping has real costs.** People take ADHD medication because unmedicated days are genuinely harder, and telling someone to just skip it as if that were free is bad advice. Work, driving, childcare: those are real reasons not to skip. This is your call, and the point of the numbers is so you can make it with actual figures instead of a guess.

**Formulation changes the maths, but do not change your prescription over this.** Immediate-release stimulants such as Adderall IR or Ritalin clear faster than Vyvanse, so a morning IR dose leaves less on board by midnight. That is a fact about pharmacokinetics, not a suggestion. Do not switch medications, split capsules, take extra, or take anyone else's prescription. If your schedule is genuinely a problem, that is a conversation with your prescriber.

## On the night

- **Do not redose MDMA.** If the roll feels weak, that is a predictable result of the combination, not a signal to take more. Our [MDMA dosing guide](/blog/mdma-dosing-guide.html) covers why redosing mostly buys side effects.
- **No alcohol, or very little.** It compounds the dehydration and heat problems this combination already worsens.
- **Take real breaks.** Twenty minutes out of the crowd, somewhere cooler, is the most effective thing you can do.
- **Water at a sensible rate**, roughly a cup an hour if dancing hard, not litres at a time. Overdrinking on MDMA causes hyponatremia.
- **Tell one person** what you took and when, including the Vyvanse.
- **Test your MDMA.** A stimulant adulterant on top of this stack is a much worse problem.

**Stop and get help for any of these**: chest pain or tightness, confusion or disorientation, a very high body temperature, skin that is hot and dry because sweating has stopped, seizure, or fainting. Hot dry skin in someone who has been dancing is a medical emergency. Get them somewhere cool, get help, and tell medical staff exactly what was taken. Festival medics and emergency departments are treating the person, not investigating them.

## The bottom line

Thirteen hours does not make a morning Vyvanse dose irrelevant by midnight, and the maths is simple enough to check yourself: roughly 40 to 50 percent of peak d-amphetamine is still on board. The closest human study of a stimulant plus MDMA found more cardiovascular and adverse effects with no more of the experience people are actually after. If you go ahead anyway, the priorities are heat, no redosing, and a friend who knows what you took.
